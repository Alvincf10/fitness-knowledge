# Fish Oil

## Overview

Fish oil provides the long-chain omega-3 fatty acids EPA and DHA. In fitness contexts it is used mainly for general health and possible recovery/inflammation support, not as a primary hypertrophy supplement.

## What It Is

Fish oil is derived from fatty fish and supplies eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA). Algal oil can supply DHA/EPA for people avoiding fish.

## Benefits

- Supports meeting omega-3 intake when fish consumption is low
- Associated with cardiovascular and general health markers in broader nutrition literature
- May modestly influence recovery-related outcomes in some athletic studies, with mixed results
- Not a substitute for progressive training or protein adequacy

## Who Should Take It

People with low fatty-fish intake who want a practical EPA/DHA source. Athletes expecting large strength or hypertrophy effects will likely be disappointed relative to creatine or protein.

## Recommended Dose

- Common supplemental targets often provide about 1–3 g/day combined EPA+DHA, product-dependent
- Dose by actual EPA+DHA content on the label, not only “fish oil” grams
- Food-first approach: fatty fish several times weekly can reduce need for capsules

## Timing

Anytime with meals is fine. Timing relative to workouts is not critical for primary outcomes.

## Side Effects

- Fishy aftertaste, burps, or GI upset
- Higher doses can affect bleeding risk in susceptible individuals or with anticoagulants
- Quality/oxidation varies by product

## Interactions

- Caution with anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy—seek clinical advice
- Compatible with standard sports supplements like creatine and protein

## Evidence Summary

Omega-3 research is extensive for general health. Sports-performance and hypertrophy-specific benefits are less consistent than for creatine, caffeine, or protein. Evidence for routine high-dose fish oil purely for muscle growth is limited; prioritize dietary pattern and proven ergogenic aids first.
