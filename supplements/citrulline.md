# Citrulline

## Overview

L-citrulline and citrulline malate are used to support nitric-oxide related blood flow, reduce fatigue, and potentially improve high-intensity training performance. Evidence is promising but less consistent than for creatine or caffeine.

## What It Is

Citrulline is a nonessential amino acid that increases arginine availability and nitric oxide production more effectively than oral arginine in many contexts. Citrulline malate combines citrulline with malate.

## Benefits

- May improve repetitions to failure and reduce perceived muscle soreness in some resistance-training studies
- Can support high-intensity exercise capacity in certain protocols
- Often used for “pump” and fatigue resistance, though subjective pump is not a hypertrophy requirement

## Who Should Take It

Lifters seeking a modest high-volume performance aid who respond well in practice. Lower priority than creatine, protein adequacy, sleep, and progressive overload.

## Recommended Dose

- L-citrulline: commonly ~3–6 g pre-workout in research and practice
- Citrulline malate: often 6–8 g (product-dependent citrulline yield)
- Use products that disclose actual L-citrulline content

## Timing

- About 60 minutes before training is common in studies
- Acute timing matters more than for creatine

## Side Effects

- Generally well tolerated at studied doses
- GI discomfort is possible at higher intakes

## Interactions

- Frequently stacked with caffeine and creatine
- Those with blood-pressure conditions or related medications should seek clinical advice before vasodilatory stacks

## Evidence Summary

Reviews show mixed-to-positive effects on high-intensity performance and training volume, with heterogeneity by dose, form, and protocol. Evidence is moderate (not as robust as creatine/caffeine). Prioritize training quality over chasing pump-focused supplements.
