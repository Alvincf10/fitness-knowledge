# Beta-Alanine

## Overview

Beta-alanine is an amino acid supplement that raises muscle carnosine, a buffer against exercise-induced acidosis. It is most useful for efforts lasting roughly 1–4 minutes and for high-volume training with strong glycolytic demand.

## What It Is

Beta-alanine is the rate-limiting precursor for carnosine synthesis in skeletal muscle. Supplemental beta-alanine chronically elevates carnosine stores.

## Benefits

- Improves performance in high-intensity efforts lasting about 1–4 minutes
- May help capacity in repeated hard sets with short rests
- Less relevant for pure one-rep max strength or long low-intensity cardio

## Who Should Take It

Athletes in middle-distance, combat, rowing, CrossFit-style, or hypertrophic training with repeated near-maximal sets who want a buffering aid. Lower priority for maximal-strength specialists with long rests.

## Recommended Dose

- About 3.2–6.4 g/day for at least 2–4 weeks to saturate carnosine
- Split into smaller servings (for example 0.8–1.6 g) to reduce paresthesia
- Maintenance dosing continues after loading/saturation

## Timing

Chronic daily intake matters more than acute pre-workout timing. Split doses across the day for comfort.

## Side Effects

- Harmless tingling (paresthesia) is common with large single doses
- Splitting doses or using slow-release forms reduces tingling

## Interactions

- Compatible with creatine and caffeine in many stacking protocols
- Not a substitute for progressive training or carbohydrate availability

## Evidence Summary

ISSN position stands and meta-analyses support beta-alanine for high-intensity exercise in the ~1–10 minute range, with clearest effects often near 1–4 minutes. Evidence is stronger than for most “pump” supplements and weaker than creatine for strength outcomes.
