# Whey Protein

## Overview

Whey protein is a fast-digesting dairy protein used to help people meet daily protein targets that support muscle repair and hypertrophy. It is a convenience tool, not a magic anabolic compound beyond its amino acid content.

## What It Is

Whey is the liquid fraction of milk protein remaining after cheese/casein separation. Common forms include concentrate, isolate, and hydrolysate. All provide high-quality essential amino acids, including leucine.

## Benefits

- Efficient way to raise daily protein intake
- Stimulates muscle protein synthesis when used within a high-protein diet
- Useful peri-workout when whole food is impractical
- Supports hypertrophy and recovery outcomes when total daily protein is otherwise insufficient

## Who Should Take It

Lifters who struggle to hit protein targets from food alone, busy trainees needing portable protein, and people who tolerate dairy proteins. Unnecessary if whole-food intake already meets needs.

## Recommended Dose

- Typical serving: 20–40 g protein depending on body size and meal distribution
- Daily target still dominates: often roughly 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day for hypertrophy-focused trainees
- Distribute protein across several feedings when possible

## Timing

Any time of day works if daily totals are met. Post-workout use is convenient but not uniquely mandatory. Pre-sleep dairy protein strategies more often feature casein, though whey still counts toward totals.

## Side Effects

- Bloating or GI discomfort in lactose-sensitive individuals (isolates are often better tolerated)
- Excess calories if used on top of an already sufficient diet during fat-loss phases

## Interactions

- Stacks well with creatine and carbohydrate around training
- Not a replacement for total energy or micronutrient adequacy
- Those with dairy allergy should avoid whey and use alternative proteins

## Evidence Summary

Meta-analyses show protein supplementation augments strength and fat-free mass gains when baseline protein is suboptimal and training is progressive. ISSN and ACSM guidance emphasize total daily protein quality and quantity; whey is simply a high-quality, practical source.
