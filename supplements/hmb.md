# HMB

## Overview

β-Hydroxy β-methylbutyrate (HMB) is a leucine metabolite studied for reducing muscle protein breakdown and supporting recovery, especially in untrained people starting hard training or in catabolic contexts. Effects in well-trained lifters on optimal diets are often modest.

## What It Is

HMB is produced endogenously from leucine metabolism and sold as calcium HMB or HMB free acid. It is classified as a sports nutrition supplement with anti-catabolic research interest.

## Benefits

- May reduce markers of muscle damage and support recovery in some novice or high-stress protocols
- Can aid fat-free mass outcomes in certain untrained or clinical-adjacent contexts
- Less consistently beneficial for trained athletes already consuming high protein and progressive programs

## Who Should Take It

Newer trainees entering demanding programs, people in aggressive deficits who cannot raise protein further, or those targeting recovery support after discussion of priorities. Lower priority than creatine and total protein for most gym goals.

## Recommended Dose

- Commonly about 3 g/day
- Often split across the day in research protocols
- Follow product labeling for free-acid versus calcium salt forms

## Timing

Daily intake over weeks matters more than a single acute pre-workout dose. Some protocols dose around training; consistency is the main requirement.

## Side Effects

- Generally well tolerated at 3 g/day in studies
- GI discomfort is uncommon but possible

## Interactions

- Does not replace dietary protein or resistance training
- Can be used alongside creatine; creatine remains the higher-yield default for strength
- Clinical populations should use clinician guidance

## Evidence Summary

Reviews show mixed results: clearer potential in untrained or catabolic settings, smaller or null effects in trained lifters with robust protein intake. ISSN literature discusses HMB as situational rather than universally essential. Evidence is moderate and context-dependent.
