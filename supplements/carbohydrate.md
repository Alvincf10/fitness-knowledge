# Carbohydrate

## Overview

Carbohydrate supplements (gels, drinks, powders) provide rapidly available fuel for training and competition. They are among the most evidence-based performance nutrition tools for glycogen-demanding sessions, not a requirement for every short lifting workout.

## What It Is

Supplemental carbohydrate includes glucose, sucrose, maltodextrin, fructose blends, and similar forms designed for rapid gastric emptying and oxidation during exercise. Whole-food carbohydrate remains foundational outside acute fueling.

## Benefits

- Improves performance in prolonged endurance and high-volume sessions that tax glycogen
- Supports high-intensity intermittent efforts when carbohydrate availability matters
- Aids glycogen restoration when rapid recovery between sessions is required
- Can make hitting energy needs easier during hard training blocks

## Who Should Take It

Endurance athletes, athletes with multiple sessions per day, and trainees doing long high-volume workouts. Optional for brief resistance sessions if daily carbohydrate from meals is already adequate.

## Recommended Dose

- During prolonged exercise: commonly ~30–60 g/hour; up to ~90 g/hour with multiple transportable carbohydrates for elite endurance needs
- Daily intake should match training load and body-size goals rather than a single fixed supplement scoop
- Peri-workout amounts of 0.5–1.2 g/kg/hour appear in recovery literature depending on urgency

## Timing

- Before and during long sessions for fueling
- After training when rapid glycogen restoration is needed
- For hypertrophy-focused lifting, total daily carbohydrate often matters more than intra-workout gels

## Side Effects

- GI distress if dose, concentration, or fructose load exceeds tolerance
- Unnecessary surplus calories during fat-loss phases if layered onto adequate meals

## Interactions

- Synergistic with electrolytes in sports drinks for long-duration work
- Works with caffeine in many endurance protocols
- Does not replace protein for muscle repair

## Evidence Summary

ACSM and ISSN guidance strongly support carbohydrate for endurance performance and glycogen management. Evidence quality is high. For purely hypertrophic resistance training, carbohydrate is helpful for fuel and training quality, but protein and progressive overload remain primary growth drivers.
