# Casein Protein

## Overview

Casein is the slow-digesting fraction of milk protein. It is used to help meet daily protein targets and to provide a prolonged amino acid release, often overnight. Like whey, its value is mainly as high-quality protein, not as a unique anabolic shortcut.

## What It Is

Casein coagulates in the stomach and digests more slowly than whey. Micellar casein is the common supplemental form; dairy foods such as cottage cheese also provide casein.

## Benefits

- Helps raise total daily protein intake
- Provides prolonged aminoacidemia useful between long gaps between meals
- Supports muscle protein synthesis as part of an adequate protein diet
- Convenient pre-sleep protein option in some research protocols

## Who Should Take It

Trainees who need additional protein and prefer slower digestion, especially before longer fasting periods overnight. Unnecessary if daily protein from mixed meals is already sufficient.

## Recommended Dose

- Typical serving: ~30–40 g protein, adjusted to body size and remaining daily needs
- Overall daily protein target still dominates outcomes
- Can be mixed with whey across the day based on preference

## Timing

- Useful at any meal gap; pre-sleep is a studied use case
- Does not outperform equal protein totals distributed across the day for long-term hypertrophy when totals are matched

## Side Effects

- Dairy intolerance or allergy issues possible
- Heavier texture may cause fullness or GI discomfort in some users

## Interactions

- Complements whey for flexible meal planning
- Works with creatine and resistance training as part of total nutrition
- Avoid if clinically advised against dairy proteins

## Evidence Summary

Protein meta-analyses support high-quality dairy proteins for hypertrophy when they help meet intake targets. Pre-sleep casein can acutely elevate overnight MPS; long-term advantages over other protein sources at equal daily intake are modest. Evidence grade is strong for protein, moderate for casein’s unique timing advantage.
