# Electrolytes

## Overview

Electrolyte supplements provide sodium, potassium, magnesium, and sometimes calcium to replace minerals lost in sweat and support fluid balance during prolonged or high-sweat training. They are situational tools, not mandatory for every gym session.

## What It Is

Electrolytes are charged minerals that help maintain fluid distribution, nerve conduction, and muscle contraction. Sports drinks and electrolyte powders vary widely in sodium content and carbohydrate co-ingestion.

## Benefits

- Supports hydration and performance in long-duration or high-sweat conditions
- Helps replace sodium losses in heavy sweaters
- Can improve fluid retention versus plain water during prolonged exercise
- May reduce risk of hyponatremia when large fluid volumes are consumed without sodium in ultra-endurance settings

## Who Should Take It

Athletes training or competing for extended periods, in heat/humidity, or with high sweat sodium losses. Most short indoor lifting sessions do not require specialized electrolyte products if meals are normal.

## Recommended Dose

- Match sodium to sweat loss and session length; many sports drinks provide roughly 300–600+ mg sodium per liter, with higher needs for salty sweaters
- Follow product labels and total daily dietary mineral intake
- For short workouts, food-based sodium and normal fluids are often enough

## Timing

- During prolonged sessions and afterward when sweat losses were large
- Pre-event sodium strategies are sometimes used in endurance sports
- Not required around every resistance-training workout

## Side Effects

- Excess intake can cause GI upset or unnecessary thirst
- Overdrinking fluids with inadequate sodium planning is a bigger endurance risk than skipping electrolytes in short gym sessions

## Interactions

- Often combined with carbohydrate for endurance fueling
- People on blood-pressure or kidney-related medical regimens need individualized clinical guidance for sodium intake

## Evidence Summary

ACSM hydration guidance supports replacing fluid and electrolytes based on sweat loss, especially for prolonged exercise. Evidence is strong for the physiological role of sodium in fluid balance; commercial product need is context-dependent rather than universal.
