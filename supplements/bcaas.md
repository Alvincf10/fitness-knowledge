# BCAAs

## Overview

Branched-chain amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, and valine) are marketed for muscle growth and fatigue reduction. When total daily protein and essential amino acid intake are already adequate, isolated BCAA supplements usually add little for hypertrophy.

## What It Is

BCAAs are three essential amino acids oxidized in skeletal muscle and involved in muscle protein metabolism. Leucine is particularly important for initiating muscle protein synthesis, but MPS still requires a full essential amino acid complement.

## Benefits

- May reduce perceived muscle soreness or fatigue in some protocols with low protein availability
- Can provide amino acids when whole protein cannot be consumed
- Unlikely to increase hypertrophy beyond sufficient complete protein intake

## Who Should Take It

Low priority for most lifters eating enough protein. Possibly situational during long fasted training with inadequate protein access, though complete protein or EAA blends are usually better.

## Recommended Dose

- Products often provide ~5–10 g BCAAs per serving; research doses vary
- Practical recommendation: prioritize 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day complete protein instead
- If used, treat as optional and secondary to food/protein powder

## Timing

Peri-workout timing is marketed heavily; for trained individuals with adequate protein meals, timing of isolated BCAAs is rarely decisive.

## Side Effects

- Generally safe at common doses
- Unnecessary calories/cost relative to benefit when protein is already high
- Unbalanced amino acid intake is not superior to complete proteins

## Interactions

- Redundant with whey, casein, or mixed meals that already supply BCAAs
- Do not replace total protein targets with BCAA-only strategies

## Evidence Summary

Reviews indicate BCAA supplements are inferior to complete proteins for stimulating a full anabolic response when used alone, and often unnecessary when daily protein is optimized. Evidence for meaningful hypertrophy benefit in protein-sufficient trainees is limited. Evidence grade is lower than whey/creatine.
