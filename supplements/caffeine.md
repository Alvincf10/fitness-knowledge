# Caffeine

## Overview

Caffeine is a well-supported ergogenic aid for endurance, high-intensity, and strength-power performance in many athletes. Effects depend on dose, habituation, timing, and individual tolerance.

## What It Is

Caffeine is a central nervous system stimulant found in coffee, tea, energy products, and anhydrous supplements. It antagonizes adenosine receptors and can alter perceived effort and neuromuscular output.

## Benefits

- Improves endurance performance in many protocols
- Can enhance high-intensity and strength-power outcomes at appropriate doses
- May reduce perceived exertion during hard efforts
- Useful as a pre-workout performance aid when sleep and anxiety are not worsened

## Who Should Take It

Trainees seeking an acute performance boost who tolerate stimulants. Less suitable for people with caffeine sensitivity, poor sleep, unmanaged anxiety, or clinician-advised stimulant restrictions.

## Recommended Dose

- Common ergogenic range: about 3–6 mg/kg body mass
- Lower doses (~2–3 mg/kg) can still help some athletes with fewer side effects
- Very high doses increase side-effect risk without reliable extra benefit

## Timing

- Typically 30–60 minutes before training or competition
- Habitual users may still benefit, though absolute effects can be blunted
- Avoid late-day doses if sleep quality declines

## Side Effects

- Jitters, elevated heart rate, anxiety, GI upset
- Sleep disruption
- Dependence and withdrawal headaches in habitual users

## Interactions

- Additive stimulant effects with other pre-workout stimulants
- May interact with certain medications; clinical advice is warranted when relevant
- Can be combined with creatine chronically; acute co-ingestion concerns are often overstated for practical dosing

## Evidence Summary

ISSN and multiple meta-analyses support caffeine as an effective ergogenic aid across endurance and many high-intensity domains. Inter-individual response varies. Evidence quality is high relative to most sports supplements.
