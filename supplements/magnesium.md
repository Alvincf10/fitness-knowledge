# Magnesium

## Overview

Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in energy metabolism, nerve function, and muscle contraction. Supplementation helps when dietary intake or status is inadequate; it is not a guaranteed sleep or pump solution for replete athletes.

## What It Is

Magnesium is a dietary mineral found in nuts, seeds, legumes, whole grains, and leafy vegetables. Supplement forms include glycinate, citrate, oxide, and others with differing absorption and GI tolerance.

## Benefits

- Corrects inadequate intake that may impair normal neuromuscular function
- May support sleep quality or muscle cramp complaints in some deficient contexts, with mixed evidence
- Supports general health roles rather than direct maximal-strength ergogenesis in replete athletes

## Who Should Take It

People with low dietary magnesium intake, high sweat losses plus poor diet quality, or clinician-identified low status. Athletes eating diverse plant-rich diets often need food first, not capsules.

## Recommended Dose

- Supplemental amounts commonly range ~200–400 mg elemental magnesium/day when used
- Stay near established upper limits unless clinically directed
- Prefer forms better tolerated GI-wise if oxide causes diarrhea

## Timing

Evening dosing is popular for convenience and sleep routines, but evidence does not require a specific peri-workout window.

## Side Effects

- Diarrhea and GI upset, especially with magnesium oxide or high doses
- Excess intake is risky for people with impaired kidney function

## Interactions

- Can interact with certain antibiotics and medications via absorption timing
- Often discussed alongside vitamin D and electrolyte strategies
- Not a replacement for sleep extension or training recovery management

## Evidence Summary

Magnesium’s physiological importance is clear; athletic performance enhancement in already sufficient athletes is less consistent. Evidence supports addressing inadequate intake more than universal ergogenic use. Food-first remains preferred.
