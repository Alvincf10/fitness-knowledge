# Sodium Bicarbonate

## Overview

Sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) is an extracellular buffer used to improve performance in high-intensity efforts that produce substantial metabolic acidosis. It is effective for some anaerobic and middle-distance style efforts but has a notable gastrointestinal side-effect profile.

## What It Is

Sodium bicarbonate is an alkalizing salt that raises blood bicarbonate and helps buffer hydrogen ions during intense exercise.

## Benefits

- Can improve performance in efforts roughly 1–7 minutes and repeated high-intensity bouts
- Supports buffering when glycolytic demand is high
- Limited relevance for pure maximal strength singles with long rests

## Who Should Take It

Athletes in middle-distance, combat, rowing, swimming repeats, or similar high-acidosis events who tolerate GI effects. Not a first-line hypertrophy supplement.

## Recommended Dose

- Commonly about 0.2–0.3 g/kg body mass taken before exercise
- Split-dose or multi-day protocols are sometimes used to reduce GI distress
- Trial in training before competition

## Timing

- Often 60–180 minutes pre-exercise depending on protocol and tolerance
- Individual experimentation is required because absorption and side effects vary

## Side Effects

- Nausea, bloating, diarrhea, and abdominal pain are common
- Sodium load is high; relevant for blood-pressure and sodium-restricted contexts
- Poor tolerance can erase any performance upside

## Interactions

- Sometimes compared or combined carefully with beta-alanine (intra- vs extracellular buffering)
- Seek clinical advice if sodium-sensitive or medically sodium-restricted
- Do not confuse household misuse or extreme dosing with studied athletic protocols

## Evidence Summary

Meta-analyses and ISSN-related sports nutrition literature support sodium bicarbonate as an evidence-based buffer for high-intensity performance, tempered by GI tolerability. Evidence grade is high for relevant event types when dosing is tolerated.
