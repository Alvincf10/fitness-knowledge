# What Is RIR?

## Question

What does reps in reserve (RIR) mean, and how should I use it?

## Short Answer

RIR estimates how many more clean reps you could do before failure. Most hypertrophy sets work well at about 0–3 RIR.

## Detailed Explanation

If you stop a set at 2 RIR, you could likely complete two more repetitions with good form. RIR helps autoregulate effort when energy and strength fluctuate. It pairs with load progression: add weight or reps when sets become easier than the target RIR. Accuracy improves with practice and occasional calibration sets.

## Scientific Evidence

RPE/RIR research shows useful estimation accuracy near failure in trained lifters. Hypertrophy evidence supports near-failure training. Evidence grade A for practical use.

## Practical Advice

- Prescribe most working sets at 0–3 RIR
- Calibrate monthly on a safe exercise by noting true failure proximity
- Leave more reps in reserve on technical heavy compounds when needed
- Log RIR beside load and reps
