# How Much Protein?

## Question

How much protein should I eat to build muscle?

## Short Answer

Most resistance trainees do well at about 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kilogram of body mass per day. Higher intakes can help during aggressive deficits, but more is not endlessly better.

## Detailed Explanation

Protein supplies essential amino acids for muscle repair and growth. Meta-analyses show benefits of higher intakes versus low intakes when people lift weights. Beyond roughly the 1.6–2.2 g/kg range, additional protein yields diminishing returns for hypertrophy if training and calories are already solid. Distribute intake across several meals when practical. Food first; powder is optional convenience.

## Scientific Evidence

Morton et al. and ISSN position stands support elevated protein for exercising populations. Evidence grade A for the practical hypertrophy range above sedentary RDAs.

## Practical Advice

- Set a daily target in the 1.6–2.2 g/kg range
- Use 3–5 protein feedings if schedule allows
- Raise intake toward the high end while cutting
- Choose whey, dairy, meat, eggs, soy, or mixed plant sources that you will actually eat
