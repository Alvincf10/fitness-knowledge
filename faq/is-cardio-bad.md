# Is Cardio Bad?

## Question

Does cardio kill muscle gains or ruin strength progress?

## Short Answer

No. Cardio is not inherently bad. Excessive high-fatigue cardio without enough recovery, calories, or lifting stimulus can interfere with hypertrophy, but moderate cardio usually helps health and fat loss.

## Detailed Explanation

The “cardio kills gains” idea comes from interference effects when endurance volume is high and recovery resources are limited. Concurrent training research shows strength and hypertrophy can still improve when resistance training is prioritized and cardio dosing is sensible. Cardio increases energy expenditure, supports heart health, and can improve work capacity. Problems arise from huge running volumes, poor protein/calorie intake, or replacing lifting with endless easy cardio.

## Scientific Evidence

Concurrent-training reviews show possible interference under high endurance loads, especially for explosive strength, but practical moderate cardio rarely abolishes hypertrophy. Fat loss still requires energy deficit. Evidence grade A that cardio is not universally harmful.

## Practical Advice

- Keep 2–4 hard lifting days as the priority for muscle goals
- Add low-to-moderate cardio for health and calorie control
- Separate hard interval cardio from key lower-body strength sessions when possible
- Raise protein and avoid huge deficits while increasing cardio
