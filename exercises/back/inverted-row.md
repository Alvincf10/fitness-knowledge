# Inverted Row

## Overview

The inverted row is a closed-chain horizontal pull performed under a bar or suspension trainer. It builds upper-back and lat strength with scalable bodyweight loading and is a key progression toward pull-ups for many beginners.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors
- Posterior chain as a plank-like stabilizer

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension musculature
- Glutes
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Smith bar, rack bar, or rings/TRX set at appropriate height
- Stable floor surface

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Set a bar at about waist-to-hip height; hang underneath with a straight body plank from heels to head.
2. Pack the shoulders; pull the chest toward the bar.
3. Squeeze scapulae at the top.
4. Lower under control to full arm extension.
5. Keep hips from sagging or piking.

## Coaching Cues

- "Body in one line"
- "Chest to bar"
- "Pack, then pull"
- "Hips do not sag"

## Common Mistakes

- Hip sag turning it into a shrug-hang
- Partial ROM
- Leading with the head only
- Bar set so high that stimulus disappears

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Arms fully extended under tension to chest-near-bar contraction.

## Progressions

- Lower the bar / elevate feet
- Weighted vest
- Pause at the top
- Ring inverted rows

## Regressions

- Higher bar angle (more upright)
- Bent-knee inverted row
- Seated cable row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent bodyweight horizontal-pull hypertrophy tool.

#### Strength

Builds relative pulling strength toward pull-ups.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep accessible.

#### Fat Loss

Great minimal-equipment pull.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
