# Gorilla Row

## Overview

The gorilla row is a hinged double-dumbbell row alternating sides from a wide stance, combining bilateral hinge bracing with unilateral rowing for lat and mid-back stimulus plus anti-rotation demand.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors
- Spinal erectors (isometric hinge)

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-rotation in the hinge
- Hip hinge stabilizers

## Equipment

- Pair of dumbbells
- Flat floor

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Hinge with a wide stance holding two dumbbells hanging below the shoulders.
2. Row one dumbbell to the hip while the other stays long as a counterbalance.
3. Lower and alternate sides.
4. Keep hips square and spine neutral.
5. Use loads that preserve the hinge.

## Coaching Cues

- "Stay hinged"
- "Alternate elbows to hips"
- "Hips quiet"
- "Long arm counterbalance"

## Common Mistakes

- Standing up out of the hinge
- Rotating the pelvis
- Rounding the back
- Short ROM

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Long hang to hip-height row finish each side.

## Progressions

- Heavier DBs
- Pause each side
- Slow eccentrics

## Regressions

- Supported single-arm DB row
- Seated cable row
- Chest-supported row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–12 per side

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Time-efficient unilateral/bilateral hybrid row.

#### Strength

Secondary.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient if hinge quality holds.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
