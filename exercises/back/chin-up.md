# Chin-Up

## Overview

The chin-up is a closed-chain vertical pull performed with a supinated (underhand) grip. Compared with a pronated pull-up, it typically increases elbow-flexor contribution while still heavily loading the latissimus dorsi and upper back. It is a foundational relative-strength and hypertrophy movement.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Elbow flexors (biceps brachii, brachialis) with elevated relative demand versus pronated pull-ups

### Secondary

- Rhomboids and middle trapezius
- Rear deltoids
- Brachioradialis

### Stabilizers

- Core musculature
- Scapular depressors and retractors
- Rotator cuff

## Equipment

- Pull-up bar
- Optional: chalk, straps (use judiciously), assistance band, or weight belt

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Hang from the bar with a supinated grip, hands roughly shoulder-width, arms extended, shoulders packed (not shrugged into the ears).
2. Initiate by depressing and retracting the scapulae, then pull the elbows down and back.
3. Continue until the chin clears the bar (or chest approaches the bar in advanced variations).
4. Lower under control to a full hang without losing shoulder packing.
5. Reset tension and repeat.

## Coaching Cues

- "Pack the shoulders, then pull"
- "Elbows to the ribs/pockets"
- "Chest up toward the bar"
- "Lower with control—no drop"

## Common Mistakes

- Kipping or excessive swing when strict reps are intended
- Incomplete ROM (chronic half-reps without programming intent)
- Shrugging into the neck at the bottom or top
- Flaring elbows without lat engagement
- Relying only on elbow flexion and losing scapular control

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

From a controlled dead hang to chin-over-bar (or chest-to-bar if capacity allows). Avoid painful end-range shoulder positions.

## Progressions

- Weighted chin-ups
- Slow eccentrics / paused reps at the top
- Chest-to-bar chin-ups
- L-sit chin-ups (advanced trunk demand)

## Regressions

- Band-assisted chin-up
- Machine-assisted chin-up
- Negative-only chin-ups
- Underhand lat pulldown

## Programming

### Sets

- Strength: 3–5 sets
- Hypertrophy: 3–4 sets

### Reps

- Strength: 3–6
- Hypertrophy: 6–12 (use assistance or added load to stay in range)

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3 for most working sets

### Rest

- Strength: 2–4 minutes
- Hypertrophy: 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3 sessions weekly within total vertical-pull volume

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent vertical-pull hypertrophy stimulus with strong biceps contribution.

#### Strength

High-value relative-strength builder; progress with added load once bodyweight sets are solid.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep sets possible with assistance or strong trainees.

#### Fat Loss

Keep as a primary pull if technique holds under fatigue; otherwise use pulldowns.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Supinated gripping may aggravate some elbow flexor tendinopathies.
