# Lat Pulldown

## Overview

The lat pulldown is a machine- or cable-based vertical pulling exercise that loads the latissimus dorsi with meaningful contribution from the biceps, rear delts, and other upper-back muscles. It is commonly used to build pulling strength and back hypertrophy when pull-ups are not yet accessible, and as a volume tool alongside bodyweight vertical pulls.

## Primary Muscles

- Latissimus dorsi
- Upper-back synergists (depending on grip and torso angle), including teres major

## Secondary Muscles

- Biceps brachii
- Rear delts / posterior shoulder complex
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius (scapular control)
- Brachialis and brachioradialis (grip-dependent)

## Equipment

- Lat pulldown station or high cable with appropriate attachment
- Common attachments: straight bar, wide bar, neutral-grip handles

## Difficulty

beginner

## Benefits

- Scalable vertical-pull loading for beginners through advanced trainees
- Useful hypertrophy stimulus for the lats when progressed with sufficient tension and ROM
- Easier load prescription than bodyweight pull-ups
- Grip and attachment options can accommodate shoulder comfort differences

## Execution

1. Adjust the thigh pad so the torso stays seated during the pull. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width unless using a specific variation.
2. Start with arms extended overhead and a slight forward lean if needed for a full stretch without excessive lumbar sway.
3. Initiate by depressing and retracting the scapulae, then pull the elbows down and back until the bar approaches the upper chest.
4. Pause briefly with control at the bottom without shrugging or yanking.
5. Return the bar under control to full elbow extension while keeping the ribs stacked and shoulders stable.

## Common Mistakes

- Using momentum and leaning excessively to finish reps
- Pulling behind the neck, which can increase shoulder stress for many lifters
- Ending the ROM at the face and never reaching a meaningful lat contraction
- Over-shrugging at the top and losing scapular control
- Excessively wide grips that reduce effective ROM and increase discomfort

## Coaching Cues

- “Drive elbows to the back pockets”
- “Chest up, ribs down”
- “Long arms at the top; controlled pull to the chest”
- “Own the eccentric”

## Programming

Use as a primary vertical pull for beginners or as accessory volume after pull-ups/chin-ups. For hypertrophy, train in moderate rep ranges with controlled eccentrics and leave roughly 0–3 RIR. Pair with a horizontal row for balanced back development.

## Sets

- 3–4 sets

## Reps

- 8–12 reps (hypertrophy default)
- 6–8 reps for heavier strength-biased sets

## Tempo

- 2-0-1-0 to 3-1-1-0

## Rest Time

- 90–150 seconds

## Variations

- Neutral-grip lat pulldown
- Close-grip lat pulldown
- Single-arm cable pulldown
- Wide-grip lat pulldown

## Regression

- Assisted pull-up machine with higher assistance
- Band-assisted flexible pulldown pattern
- Seated cable row (if vertical pulling is poorly tolerated)

## Progression

- Heavier load with unchanged ROM
- Slow eccentrics
- Single-arm overload
- Transition toward pull-up variations

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement during overhead positions, recent lat or biceps strain, or elbow tendinopathy aggravated by pulling. Behind-the-neck variations are often poorly tolerated and are usually unnecessary. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.

## Science Summary

Vertical pulling exercises meaningfully activate the latissimus dorsi and elbow flexors. Lat pulldowns allow precise external loading and can produce similar regional back development goals as pull-ups when effort and volume are matched sufficiently, though the kinetic chain and stability demands differ. Grip width and orientation change muscle emphasis and joint stress more than they create unique “must-do” patterns; front pulldowns to the chest are generally preferred over behind-the-neck versions for comfort and practical ROM. Progressive overload and weekly pulling volume remain the primary drivers of hypertrophy.
