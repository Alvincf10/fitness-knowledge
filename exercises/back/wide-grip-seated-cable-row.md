# Wide-Grip Seated Cable Row

## Overview

The wide-grip seated cable row uses a wider pronated handle to bias upper-back horizontal abduction/retraction patterns, often increasing rear-delt and trapezius demand relative to a narrow V-handle row.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Rhomboids and middle trapezius
- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Biceps
- Posterior shoulder musculature

### Stabilizers

- Core
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Seated cable row
- Wide lat-bar or straight bar attachment
- Foot platform

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Attach a wide bar; sit with feet braced and arms extended.
2. Pull the bar to the upper abdomen/lower chest with elbows traveling out/back as appropriate.
3. Emphasize scapular retraction.
4. Return to a controlled stretch.
5. Limit torso rock.

## Coaching Cues

- "Wide hands, elbows sweep back"
- "Pinch the shoulder blades"
- "Tall chest"
- "No big rock"

## Common Mistakes

- Turning into an upright row shrug
- Excessive momentum
- Over-rounding the back on the reach
- Pulling too high into the neck

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Long-arm stretch to a retracted finish near the torso.

## Progressions

- Load
- Face-pull pairings
- Chest-supported wide row

## Regressions

- Narrow V-handle row
- Machine row
- Band pull-apart as primer

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Upper-back biased horizontal pull.

#### Strength

Secondary pulling strength.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory/compound hybrid.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
