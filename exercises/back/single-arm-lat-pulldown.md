# Single-Arm Lat Pulldown

## Overview

The single-arm lat pulldown is a unilateral vertical pull that allows independent loading, greater freedom of elbow path, and correction of side-to-side discrepancies in lat engagement.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Elbow flexors
- Rear deltoids
- Rhomboids

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-rotation musculature
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Single cable or unilateral pulldown handle
- Seat or kneeling stance
- Optional thigh support if using a standard station

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Grip a single handle overhead; stabilize the torso.
2. Pull the elbow down toward the ribcage until the hand reaches near the shoulder/chest line.
3. Emphasize lat contraction without excessive rotation.
4. Return to a full stretch overhead.
5. Complete all reps, then switch sides (or alternate).

## Coaching Cues

- "Elbow to back pocket"
- "Stretch tall"
- "Ribs down; do not twist wildly"
- "Match both sides"

## Common Mistakes

- Rotating the torso to move the stack
- Cutting the stretch
- Shrugging
- Unequal ROM between sides

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full unilateral overhead stretch to a strong side contraction.

## Progressions

- Load
- Slow eccentrics
- Kneeling single-arm pulldown with longer stretch

## Regressions

- Bilateral pulldown
- Assisted pull-up
- Straight-arm pulldown

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets per side

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds between sides/sets

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent unilateral lat hypertrophy tool.

#### Strength

Secondary; helps balance sides.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient accessory after bilateral pulls.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
