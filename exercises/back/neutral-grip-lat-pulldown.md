# Neutral-Grip Lat Pulldown

## Overview

The neutral-grip lat pulldown uses parallel handles to train vertical pulling with a grip that many lifters find comfortable on the shoulders and elbows, while loading the lats and upper back.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Elbow flexors
- Rhomboids and trapezius
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers
- Core against pad

## Equipment

- Lat pulldown station
- Neutral-grip attachment
- Thigh pad

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Grip neutral handles; secure thighs.
2. Begin arms long overhead.
3. Pull elbows down until hands reach upper chest.
4. Control the return to full stretch.
5. Keep shoulders from shrugging into ears.

## Coaching Cues

- "Neutral hands, elbows down"
- "Chest up to the handle"
- "Full stretch"
- "Pack the finish"

## Common Mistakes

- Momentum/lean cheating
- Partial stretch
- Elbow-dominant curling
- Neck jutting forward

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Overhead stretch to upper-chest contraction.

## Progressions

- Load
- Neutral-grip pull-up
- Paused reps

## Regressions

- Assisted pull-up
- Lighter pulldown
- Inverted row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Joint-friendly vertical-pull hypertrophy staple.

#### Strength

Supports pull-up strength development.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient machine pull.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
