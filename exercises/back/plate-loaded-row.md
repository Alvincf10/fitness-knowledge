# Plate-Loaded Row

## Overview

The plate-loaded row (often iso-lateral) allows independent arm loading on a guided path, combining heavy overload potential with machine stability for lat and mid-back development.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers; chest pad support if present

## Equipment

- Plate-loaded row machine
- Weight plates
- Adjustable seat/pad

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Set seat so arms reach a full stretch at the bottom.
2. Load plates evenly (or unevenly for intentional unilateral emphasis).
3. Row to a strong contraction; control the eccentric.
4. Keep ribs/chest stable against the pad.
5. Match depth side to side.

## Coaching Cues

- "Stretch first"
- "Drive elbows back"
- "Even or intentional uneven loading"
- "No pad peel"

## Common Mistakes

- Seat too far forward/back
- Slamming plates
- Asymmetry without intent
- Short ROM

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full stretch to peak contraction.

## Progressions

- More plates
- Pauses
- Single-arm emphasis

## Regressions

- Selectorized machine row
- Cable row
- Inverted row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent heavy machine hypertrophy option.

#### Strength

Strong guided overload tool.

#### Endurance

Moderate-high reps workable.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
