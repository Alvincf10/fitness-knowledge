# Scapular Pull-Up

## Overview

The scapular pull-up is a partial-range drill performed from a dead hang by depressing and retracting the scapulae without bending the elbows. It teaches shoulder packing and initiates lat engagement for pull-up progressions and scapular control.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi (initiation)
- Lower trapezius and scapular depressors

### Secondary

- Rhomboids
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Rotator cuff
- Core (light)

## Equipment

- Pull-up bar

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Hang with arms straight and relaxed shoulders (active awareness, not pain).
2. Without bending elbows, depress and retract the scapulae to lift the body slightly.
3. Hold briefly at the packed position.
4. Return to a controlled hang.
5. Repeat for crisp, small-range reps.

## Coaching Cues

- "Long arms, move the shoulder blades"
- "Pockets the shoulders down"
- "No elbow bend"
- "Quiet, precise reps"

## Common Mistakes

- Bending the elbows into a mini pull-up
- Shrugging up instead of depressing
- Using momentum
- Overdoing volume with irritated shoulders

## Breathing

Exhale into the pack; inhale as you return to hang.

## Range of Motion

Small vertical displacement via scapular motion only; elbows remain extended.

## Progressions

- Longer pauses
- Add to pull-up freestanding sets as primers
- Full pull-up progression

## Regressions

- Scapular setting on an inverted row
- Band-assisted scapular pulls
- Prone scapular retraction drills

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- Technique-focused; RIR not primary

### Rest

- 45–75 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–4x weekly as a primer or skill drill

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Accessory/skill; not a primary mass builder.

#### Strength

Improves pull-up initiation mechanics.

#### Endurance

Can be used in warm-ups.

#### Fat Loss

Low-cost primer before pulling.

## Contraindications

Avoid if hanging aggravates shoulders; use supported regressions. Not medical advice.
