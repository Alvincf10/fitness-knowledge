# Ring Row

## Overview

The ring row is an inverted row performed on gymnastic rings, adding instability that increases demand on scapular stabilizers, grip, and trunk control while training the lats and upper back.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors
- Serratus/scapular stabilizers

### Stabilizers

- Rotator cuff
- Core anti-extension
- Grip

## Equipment

- Gymnastic rings or suspension trainer
- Stable anchors
- Adjustable strap length for difficulty

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set rings at an appropriate height; hold and lean back into a plank.
2. Pack shoulders; pull rings toward the ribcage/chest.
3. Squeeze scapulae; keep rings stable.
4. Lower to full arm extension under control.
5. Shorten straps or walk feet forward to progress.

## Coaching Cues

- "Break the rings apart lightly"
- "Body rigid"
- "Pull rings to ribs"
- "Quiet rings"

## Common Mistakes

- Sagging hips
- Letting rings crash inward without control
- Partial ROM
- Angle so easy stimulus is minimal

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full extension to strong retraction/pull.

## Progressions

- More horizontal body angle
- Feet elevated
- Pause reps
- Archer ring rows (advanced)

## Regressions

- More upright angle
- Bar inverted row
- Seated cable row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

High-quality unstable horizontal pull.

#### Strength

Builds stability and relative strength.

#### Endurance

Adjust angle for higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Portable pulling option.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
