# Band-Assisted Pull-Up

## Overview

The band-assisted pull-up uses a looped resistance band to provide accommodating assistance (more help at the bottom, less near the top), bridging bodyweight vertical pulling skill and strength.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Elbow flexors
- Upper-back retractors
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Pull-up bar
- Heavy loop resistance band anchored securely over the bar
- Knee or foot in the band depending on setup

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Anchor the band safely over the bar; place a knee or foot into the loop.
2. Grip the bar and assume a packed hang.
3. Pull to chin-over-bar with minimal swing.
4. Lower under control against the band.
5. Progress by using a lighter band over time.

## Coaching Cues

- "Band is stable before you load it"
- "Strict pull"
- "Chin over, then control down"
- "Lighter band as you improve"

## Common Mistakes

- Unstable band setup (safety risk)
- Excessive kipping
- Band so heavy that top lockout has zero challenge
- Incomplete hang

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Hang to chin-over-bar.

## Progressions

- Lighter band
- Unassisted pull-up
- Pause at the top with band

## Regressions

- Heavier band
- Assisted machine
- Lat pulldown

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–3x weekly while building pull-ups

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Productive vertical-pull volume with accessible setup.

#### Strength

Progression tool toward unassisted pulling strength.

#### Endurance

Good for higher-rep practice.

#### Fat Loss

Convenient when a machine is unavailable.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Ensure band and anchor integrity before each set.
