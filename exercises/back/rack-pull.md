# Rack Pull

## Overview

The rack pull is a partial-range deadlift variation starting from pins/blocks above the floor (often below or above the knee). It overloads the upper back, traps, grip, and lockout portion of the hinge while reducing demand from the floor pull.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Spinal erectors
- Trapezius and upper back
- Latissimus dorsi (stabilizing/pulling the bar into the body)

### Secondary

- Gluteus maximus and hamstrings (depending on start height)
- Grip/forearm musculature
- Quadriceps contribution varies with height

### Stabilizers

- Core bracing musculature
- Scapular depressors/retractors
- Hip stabilizers

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Power rack with safety pins or sturdy blocks
- Optional straps depending on grip goals

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set pins at the desired height; place the bar on the pins.
2. Hinge and grip; pack the back and brace as in a deadlift.
3. Drive to lockout by extending hips and knees; stand tall without hyperextending the lumbar spine.
4. Lower under control to the pins; reset each rep (or touch-and-go if programmed).
5. Keep the bar close to the body.

## Coaching Cues

- "Brace before it leaves the pins"
- "Bar close"
- "Stand tall—do not lean back"
- "Reset tightness each rep"

## Common Mistakes

- Starting height so high it becomes a pointless shrug
- Hyperextending the low back at lockout
- Rounded thoracic/lumbar positions under maximal loads
- Bouncing violently off pins

## Breathing

Brace with a breath before the pull; exhale at lockout or between reps as appropriate for heavy sets.

## Range of Motion

From pin height to full hip extension lockout; intentionally partial versus floor deadlift.

## Progressions

- Heavier loads
- Lower pin heights toward floor pulls
- Paused lockouts

## Regressions

- Higher pins with lighter technique focus
- Chest-supported rows for upper-back volume
- Trap-bar deadlift if available (legs folder)

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–5 sets

### Reps

- Strength: 3–5
- Hypertrophy/overload: 5–8

### Intensity

- Often heavy; RIR 1–3 with conservative spinal positions

### Rest

- 2–5 minutes

### Frequency

- 1x weekly for most

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Overload stimulus for upper back/traps and posterior chain lockout.

#### Strength

Common lockout and overload strength tool.

#### Endurance

Not ideal.

#### Fat Loss

Demanding; reduce frequency in deficits.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. High spinal loading—use conservative progression and technique standards.
