# Cable Pullover

## Overview

The cable pullover (often kneeling or standing with a rope/bar from a high pulley) trains shoulder extension under continuous tension, isolating the lats more consistently than many free-weight pullover setups.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Teres major
- Triceps long head
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension
- Scapular depressors

## Equipment

- High cable
- Rope or bar
- Optional pad for kneeling

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Kneel or stand facing away/side-on depending on setup; hold the rope overhead with soft elbows.
2. Extend the shoulders to bring the rope toward the hips/thighs.
3. Squeeze the lats at the finish.
4. Return to a long stretch.
5. Keep elbows angle relatively fixed.

## Coaching Cues

- "Long arms"
- "Pull to the hips with the lats"
- "Ribs down"
- "Stretch back up"

## Common Mistakes

- Heavy elbow bend
- Lumbar flare
- Momentum
- Cutting the stretch

## Breathing

Exhale on the pull-down arc; inhale on the return.

## Range of Motion

Overhead stretch to hip-level contraction.

## Progressions

- Load
- Slow eccentrics
- Single-arm cable pullover

## Regressions

- Straight-arm pulldown with lighter stack
- Machine pullover
- Band pullover

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Continuous-tension lat isolation.

#### Strength

Not primary.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory after compounds.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
