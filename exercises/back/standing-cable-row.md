# Standing Cable Row

## Overview

The standing cable row is a horizontal pull performed in a staggered or athletic stance, increasing core demand versus seated rows while loading the lats and upper back under continuous tension.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension/anti-rotation
- Hip/leg bracing in stance

## Equipment

- Adjustable cable at mid-torso height
- Handle or bar
- Clear space for stance

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set cable mid-height; take a staggered stance and brace.
2. Start arm(s) long with packed shoulders.
3. Row elbows back to the torso.
4. Return to stretch without losing the stance brace.
5. Keep ribs down—avoid turning into a hip hinge yank.

## Coaching Cues

- "Own the stance"
- "Elbows back"
- "Tall chest"
- "No torso flop"

## Common Mistakes

- Using hip drive as the prime mover
- Rounding under fatigue
- Partial stretch
- Stance too narrow

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Long-arm stretch to elbows-back finish.

## Progressions

- Load
- Single-arm standing row
- Slow eccentrics

## Regressions

- Seated cable row
- Chest-supported row
- Machine row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Horizontal pull with extra trunk demand.

#### Strength

Secondary.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep workable.

#### Fat Loss

Good when you want combined pull + brace work.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
