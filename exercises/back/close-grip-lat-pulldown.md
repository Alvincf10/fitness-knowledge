# Close-Grip Lat Pulldown

## Overview

The close-grip lat pulldown uses a narrow attachment (often V-bar or close pronated/neutral grip), typically increasing elbow-flexor involvement and allowing a strong contraction at the bottom of the vertical pull.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Biceps brachii and brachialis
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers
- Core against pad

## Equipment

- Lat pulldown station
- V-bar or close-grip handle
- Thigh pad

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Attach a close-grip handle; secure thighs.
2. Start tall with arms extended.
3. Pull the handle to the upper chest/sternum while elbows track down and back.
4. Squeeze briefly; return to a full stretch.
5. Keep ribs down—avoid excessive lumbar arching.

## Coaching Cues

- "Pull to the chest"
- "Elbows to pockets"
- "Stretch tall at the top"
- "No bounce"

## Common Mistakes

- Turning into a biceps-only curl
- Cutting the overhead stretch
- Extreme lean and momentum
- Shrugging the finish

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full stretch overhead to a controlled peak contraction at the chest.

## Progressions

- Load
- Slow eccentrics
- Neutral-grip pull-ups

## Regressions

- Higher seat assistance via lighter load
- Assisted pull-up
- Seated cable row for horizontal volume

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Strong lat + elbow-flexor vertical-pull option.

#### Strength

Useful accessory for pulling strength.

#### Endurance

Higher reps suitable.

#### Fat Loss

Good machine compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
