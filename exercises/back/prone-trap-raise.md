# Prone Trap Raise

## Overview

The prone trap raise (prone Y raise) is a light upper-back and lower-trapezius accessory performed face-down, raising the arms into a Y pattern to improve scapular control and balance heavy pulling programs.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Lower trapezius
- Middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Rotator cuff musculature

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers
- Core against the bench

## Equipment

- Incline or flat bench
- Very light dumbbells or no load

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Lie prone on an incline/flat bench holding light dumbbells.
2. Raise arms into a Y overhead while depressing/retracting scapulae.
3. Keep thumbs up; avoid shrugging into the ears aggressively.
4. Lower under control.
5. Prioritize crisp scapular motion over load.

## Coaching Cues

- "Light weight, perfect scapulae"
- "Thumbs up into a Y"
- "Long neck"
- "Squeeze lower traps"

## Common Mistakes

- Too much load
- Turning into a shrug-heavy pattern
- Bending elbows excessively
- Neck strain from looking up hard

## Breathing

Exhale on the raise; inhale on the lower.

## Range of Motion

Arms hang under control to an overhead Y without pain.

## Progressions

- Slightly heavier micro-loads
- Longer pauses
- Face-pull pairings

## Regressions

- No-load prone Y
- Band pull-aparts
- Scapular drills on rings

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–3 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- Technique-focused RIR 2–4

### Rest

- 45–75 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–3x weekly as accessory

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Small muscle accessory; supports upper-back balance.

#### Strength

Scapular control for pulling strength.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep suitable.

#### Fat Loss

Low-fatigue accessory.

## Contraindications

Stop if shoulder pain appears; reduce ROM/load. Not medical advice.
