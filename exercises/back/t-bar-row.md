# T-Bar Row

## Overview

The T-bar row is a hinged horizontal pull using a landmine/T-bar setup or dedicated T-bar station. It allows heavy loading of the lats and mid-back with a fixed bar path that many lifters find stable compared with a free barbell row.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors
- Spinal erectors (isometric)

### Stabilizers

- Core and hip hinge stabilizers
- Scapular retractors
- Grip musculature

## Equipment

- T-bar row station or barbell in landmine with handle attachment
- Weight plates
- Optional chest pad if machine provides one

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Straddle the bar; hinge to a stable torso angle with neutral spine.
2. Grip handles; start with arms long and shoulders packed.
3. Row by driving elbows back until the plates/handles approach the torso.
4. Squeeze the back; lower under control to a stretch.
5. Keep hips and torso angle relatively consistent—avoid standing upright each rep.

## Coaching Cues

- "Hinge and brace"
- "Elbows back"
- "Chest tall, not standing up"
- "Control the stretch"

## Common Mistakes

- Turning the set into a shrug-deadlift hybrid
- Rounding the lumbar spine
- Using plates that block ROM
- Yanking with hip extension every rep

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Long-arm stretch to elbows-back contraction without losing hinge posture.

## Progressions

- Heavier load
- Paused rows
- Chest-supported T-bar if available

## Regressions

- Chest-supported row
- Seated cable row
- Dumbbell row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Heavy mid-back and lat builder.

#### Strength

Strong overload row option.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Keep if hinge position stays crisp when fatigued.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
