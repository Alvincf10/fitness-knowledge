# Barbell Shrug

## Overview

The barbell shrug isolates scapular elevation primarily for the upper trapezius. It is included in back programming for upper-back thickness and trap development, typically as a short accessory after heavier pulls.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Upper trapezius

### Secondary

- Levator scapulae
- Middle trapezius (stabilizing)

### Stabilizers

- Core bracing
- Grip musculature

## Equipment

- Barbell (dumbbell/trap-bar variations exist)
- Optional straps

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Stand tall holding a barbell at the thighs with arms long.
2. Elevate the scapulae straight up toward the ears without rolling.
3. Pause briefly at the top.
4. Lower under control to a full scapular depression stretch.
5. Keep elbows extended—do not turn it into an upright row.

## Coaching Cues

- "Up, not roll"
- "Arms long"
- "Pause at the top"
- "Full lower between reps"

## Common Mistakes

- Rolling the shoulders excessively
- Bending elbows into upright rows
- Using so much load that ROM disappears
- Neck craning

## Breathing

Exhale on the elevation; inhale on the lower.

## Range of Motion

Full scapular depression to full elevation without pain.

## Progressions

- Heavier load
- Longer pauses
- Dumbbell/trap-bar shrug variations

## Regressions

- Lighter shrugs
- Face-pull and upper-back rows for balanced development

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Upper-trap accessory hypertrophy.

#### Strength

Secondary; supports isometric holding strength for pulls.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep suitable.

#### Fat Loss

Optional low-skill accessory.

## Contraindications

Avoid aggressive loading with neck pain; use controlled ROM. Not medical advice.
