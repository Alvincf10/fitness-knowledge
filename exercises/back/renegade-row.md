# Renegade Row

## Overview

The renegade row is a plank-based alternating dumbbell row that combines horizontal pulling with high anti-rotation and anti-extension core demand. Back loading is typically lighter than dedicated rows because stability limits the weight.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids (moderate loads)

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors
- Core anti-rotation musculature (high demand)

### Stabilizers

- Full plank chain: abs, glutes, shoulders
- Base-side shoulder stabilizers

## Equipment

- Pair of hexagonal dumbbells (preferred for stability)
- Flat floor

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Assume a plank on two dumbbells, feet wide for stability.
2. Row one dumbbell to the hip while keeping hips square.
3. Lower it; alternate sides.
4. Minimize rotation and hip sway.
5. Use loads that preserve plank quality.

## Coaching Cues

- "Wide feet, tight plank"
- "Hips stay quiet"
- "Elbow to hip"
- "Light enough to own the position"

## Common Mistakes

- Rotating the pelvis each rep
- Sagging lumbar spine
- Ego loading
- Hands on round dumbbells that roll (safety)

## Breathing

Exhale on each row; maintain brace throughout.

## Range of Motion

Arm long on the floor support side; working arm to hip-height row finish.

## Progressions

- Heavier DBs within control
- Pause at the top
- Narrower feet (harder anti-rotation)

## Regressions

- Plank with single-arm DB row hand on bench
- Standard dumbbell row
- Bird-dog row regressions

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–10 per side

### Intensity

- RIR 2–4 (stability-limited)

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Secondary back stimulus; more core-stability hybrid.

#### Strength

Anti-rotation strength emphasis.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Conditioning-friendly hybrid if technique holds.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Avoid if plank positions aggravate wrists/shoulders/low back.
