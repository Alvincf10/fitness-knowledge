# Straight-Arm Pulldown

## Overview

The straight-arm pulldown is a lat-isolation pattern performed with nearly extended elbows, emphasizing shoulder extension under cable tension. It is useful for teaching lat engagement and adding lengthened- or shortened-position lat work without heavy elbow-flexor fatigue.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Teres major
- Rear deltoids
- Triceps long head (as a shoulder extensor contributor)

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension
- Scapular depressors

## Equipment

- High cable
- Straight bar, lat bar, or rope

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Stand facing a high cable; grip the bar with arms nearly straight.
2. Hinge slightly; brace the trunk.
3. Sweep the bar down toward the thighs using shoulder extension (lats), keeping elbows softly locked.
4. Squeeze at the bottom; return under control to a stretch overhead/in front.
5. Avoid turning the movement into a triceps pushdown with heavy elbow bend.

## Coaching Cues

- "Soft elbows stay soft"
- "Push the bar to the thighs with the lats"
- "Ribs down"
- "Stretch up, squeeze down"

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive elbow bend (becomes a pulldown/pushdown hybrid)
- Lumbar overarching
- Using momentum
- Shrugging

## Breathing

Exhale as the bar descends; inhale on the return stretch.

## Range of Motion

From a long overhead/front stretch to bar-near-thigh peak contraction.

## Progressions

- Load
- Slow eccentrics
- Single-arm straight-arm pulldown

## Regressions

- Lighter cable
- Machine pullover
- Band straight-arm pulldown

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

High-value lat isolation accessory.

#### Strength

Not a primary strength lift.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep suitable.

#### Fat Loss

Low-skill accessory after compounds.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
