# Negative Pull-Up

## Overview

The negative pull-up emphasizes the eccentric phase by starting at the top (jump or step to chin-over-bar) and lowering slowly. Eccentric-focused practice builds pull-up strength and control when concentric pull-ups are not yet achievable.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Elbow flexors
- Rhomboids and trapezius
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Pull-up bar
- Box or step to reach the top position
- Optional band for partial assistance

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Step or jump to a chin-over-bar position with shoulders packed.
2. Lower for a prescribed tempo (often 3–5+ seconds) to a full hang.
3. Drop or step down safely; reset for the next rep.
4. Keep the body from uncontrolled falling.
5. Stop if scapular control is lost.

## Coaching Cues

- "Start strong at the top"
- "Fight the descent"
- "Shoulders packed all the way down"
- "Smooth, not a freefall"

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping too fast
- Losing packing and dumping into the shoulders
- Too many hard eccentrics causing excessive soreness without recovery
- Incomplete top starting position

## Breathing

Inhale and brace at the top; controlled exhale during the slow descent as needed.

## Range of Motion

Chin-over-bar start to controlled full hang.

## Progressions

- Longer eccentric times
- Pause mid-ROM
- Assisted concentric + negative
- Full pull-up

## Regressions

- Shorter eccentrics
- Lat pulldown slow eccentrics
- Inverted row slow lowers

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–5 sets

### Reps

- 3–6 slow negatives

### Intensity

- High effort; leave recovery buffer (not daily maxing)

### Rest

- 2–3 minutes

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly depending on recovery

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Eccentric loading can support hypertrophy while building pull-up capacity.

#### Strength

Key bridge for pull-up strength development.

#### Endurance

Not ideal as high-rep endurance work.

#### Fat Loss

Use sparingly if sore; prefer pulldowns when fatigued.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. High eccentric stress—manage volume carefully.
