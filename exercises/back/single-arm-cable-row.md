# Single-Arm Cable Row

## Overview

The single-arm cable row is a unilateral horizontal pull that trains the lats and upper back while challenging anti-rotation core stability and allowing independent ROM per side.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-rotation musculature
- Scapular stabilizers
- Glutes/hips in staggered stance variations

## Equipment

- Single adjustable cable
- D-handle
- Optional bench or standing staggered stance

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Set cable around mid-torso height; stagger stance or sit supported.
2. Start arm long with shoulder packed.
3. Row the elbow back toward the hip/ribcage.
4. Squeeze the shoulder blade; return to stretch without twisting excessively.
5. Switch sides and match effort/ROM.

## Coaching Cues

- "Elbow to hip"
- "Square the torso"
- "Reach long, then row"
- "Do not rotate to cheat"

## Common Mistakes

- Rotating through the lumbar spine to move load
- Shrugging
- Short ROM
- Uneven sides

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full unilateral stretch to elbow-back contraction.

## Progressions

- Load
- Slow eccentrics
- Standing unsupported variations

## Regressions

- Seated bilateral cable row
- Chest-supported dumbbell row
- Machine single-arm row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets per side

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

High-quality unilateral back hypertrophy.

#### Strength

Helps address asymmetries.

#### Endurance

Higher reps suitable.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient accessory.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
