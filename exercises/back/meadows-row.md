# Meadows Row

## Overview

The Meadows row is a unilateral landmine row performed with a staggered stance and an overhand grip on the bar sleeve, emphasizing lat stretch and upper-back thickness through a long ROM.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors
- Obliques (anti-rotation)

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-rotation
- Hip hinge stabilizers
- Grip on the sleeve

## Equipment

- Barbell in a landmine attachment
- Optional handle adapter for comfort
- Plates

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Stand perpendicular to the bar; hinge and grip the sleeve with the near hand.
2. Start with a long stretch across the lat.
3. Row the elbow back/out as the bar travels toward the hip/torso.
4. Lower into a deep stretch without rotating the pelvis excessively.
5. Finish the set, then switch sides.

## Coaching Cues

- "Long stretch, then elbow back"
- "Hips square"
- "Do not twist to yank"
- "Squeeze at the top"

## Common Mistakes

- Rotating through the lumbar spine
- Cutting the stretch
- Using too much load and losing hinge position
- Shrugging

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Deep unilateral stretch to strong elbow-back contraction.

## Progressions

- Load
- Slow stretch eccentrics
- Pause at peak contraction

## Regressions

- Single-arm cable row
- Dumbbell row
- Chest-supported single-arm row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets per side

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent unilateral lat/upper-back hypertrophy variation.

#### Strength

Secondary unilateral strength.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
