# Reverse-Grip Lat Pulldown

## Overview

The reverse-grip lat pulldown uses a supinated grip on the pulldown bar, increasing elbow-flexor demand similarly to chin-ups while allowing precise load selection for lat and biceps-biased vertical pulling.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Biceps brachii and brachialis
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers
- Core against pad

## Equipment

- Lat pulldown station
- Straight bar
- Thigh pad

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Grip the bar underhand at about shoulder width; secure thighs.
2. Start with arms extended.
3. Pull the bar to the upper chest with elbows tracking down/back.
4. Squeeze; return to full stretch under control.
5. Avoid excessive lumbar arching.

## Coaching Cues

- "Underhand, elbows to ribs"
- "Bar to chest"
- "Control the stretch"
- "Do not turn it into a pure curl"

## Common Mistakes

- Curl-dominant pattern with no scapular motion
- Too-narrow grip stressing elbows
- Momentum
- Incomplete stretch

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full overhead stretch to upper-chest contact.

## Progressions

- Load
- Chin-up progressions
- Slow eccentrics

## Regressions

- Neutral-grip pulldown
- Assisted chin-up
- Seated row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Lat + biceps biased vertical pull.

#### Strength

Accessory toward chin-up strength.

#### Endurance

Higher reps OK if elbows tolerate.

#### Fat Loss

Useful machine compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Monitor medial elbow discomfort.
