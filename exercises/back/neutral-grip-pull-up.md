# Neutral-Grip Pull-Up

## Overview

The neutral-grip pull-up uses a parallel (palms-facing) grip, often feeling more shoulder- and elbow-friendly than fully pronated or supinated variations while still loading the lats, upper back, and elbow flexors.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Biceps brachii and brachialis
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core musculature
- Scapular stabilizers
- Rotator cuff

## Equipment

- Neutral-grip pull-up handles or parallel bars
- Optional assistance or weight belt

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Grip neutral handles; hang with arms extended and shoulders packed.
2. Depress/retract scapulae, then pull elbows down.
3. Raise until chin clears the handles.
4. Lower under control to a full hang.
5. Minimize swing unless kipping is intentionally programmed.

## Coaching Cues

- "Palms face each other"
- "Pull elbows to pockets"
- "Pack, then pull"
- "Quiet body, strict reps"

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive swinging
- Partial ROM
- Shrugging
- Neck craning over the bar without scapular control

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Dead hang to chin-over-handles with controlled scapular motion.

## Progressions

- Weighted neutral-grip pull-ups
- Slow eccentrics
- Chest-to-bar neutral pulls

## Regressions

- Assisted neutral-grip pull-up
- Neutral-grip lat pulldown
- Inverted row with neutral handles

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–5 sets

### Reps

- 4–12 depending on goal and assistance/load

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds (up to 3–4 minutes for heavy weighted sets)

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Joint-friendly vertical pull for lat and upper-back volume.

#### Strength

Strong relative-strength option when pronated grip irritates tissues.

#### Endurance

Assistance enables higher-rep work.

#### Fat Loss

Solid primary pull when recovered.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
