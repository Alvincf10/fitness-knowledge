# Chest-Supported Row

## Overview

The chest-supported row (machine or bench-supported free weights) removes much of the lower-back demand from rowing, allowing focused loading of the upper and mid-back and lats with less systemic fatigue from hip hinging.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers
- Minimal lumbar demand versus bent-over rows

## Equipment

- Chest-supported row machine, or incline bench with dumbbells/barbell
- Appropriate handles

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Set chest pad/bench so arms can hang freely at the bottom.
2. Grab handles or dumbbells; start at a full stretch.
3. Row by driving elbows back and retracting scapulae.
4. Lower under control to stretch.
5. Keep ribs against the pad—avoid peeling the chest off to cheat.

## Coaching Cues

- "Chest glued to the pad"
- "Elbows back"
- "Squeeze at the top"
- "Full stretch at the bottom"

## Common Mistakes

- Peeling the torso off the pad
- Shrugging
- Partial stretch
- Yanking with momentum

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full hang/stretch to strong retraction without lumbar compensation.

## Progressions

- Load
- Pauses
- Single-arm chest-supported row

## Regressions

- Lighter machine row
- Seated cable row with lighter load
- Band row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent high-quality back volume with lower spinal fatigue.

#### Strength

Secondary strength; great accessory volume tool.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Useful when low-back fatigue is limiting.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
