# Machine Pullover

## Overview

The machine pullover uses a guided arc to load shoulder extension for the latissimus dorsi with supported positioning, reducing skill demand versus free-weight pullovers while providing consistent tension.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Pectoralis major (machine-dependent)
- Triceps long head
- Teres major

### Stabilizers

- Scapular musculature against pads
- Minimal compared with free standing cables

## Equipment

- Pullover machine
- Adjustable seat and arm pads per design

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Adjust seat so the shoulder aligns with the machine axis.
2. Place elbows on pads (or grip handles as designed).
3. Move through the arc from stretch to contraction under control.
4. Avoid forcing painful end ranges.
5. Return slowly without slamming.

## Coaching Cues

- "Align the shoulders to the axis"
- "Sweep through the arc"
- "Control the stretch"
- "Do not bounce"

## Common Mistakes

- Poor seat alignment
- Excessive load
- Using momentum
- Ignoring shoulder discomfort at end range

## Breathing

Exhale toward contraction; inhale toward stretch.

## Range of Motion

Machine-available stretch to contraction within comfort.

## Progressions

- Load
- Tempo
- Cable pullover free-path progressions

## Regressions

- Straight-arm pulldown light
- Shorter ROM
- Band pullover

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Supported lat isolation volume.

#### Strength

Not primary.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep suitable.

#### Fat Loss

Optional accessory.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
