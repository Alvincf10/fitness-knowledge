# Kroc Row

## Overview

The Kroc row is a high-effort, high-rep unilateral dumbbell row performed with heavier loads and some controlled body English historically used for upper-back thickness, grip, and work capacity. Modern coaching still prioritizes a relatively stable spine and full ROM.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle/upper trapezius

### Secondary

- Elbow flexors
- Rear deltoids
- Grip/forearm musculature

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-rotation
- Hip hinge support
- Grip endurance

## Equipment

- Heavy dumbbell
- Bench for support
- Optional straps (debate: use based on grip goals)

## Difficulty

advanced

## Execution

1. Brace one arm on a bench; hinge with a neutral spine.
2. Row a challenging dumbbell for higher reps, allowing minimal controlled momentum only if positions stay safe.
3. Emphasize a long stretch and strong finish each rep.
4. Stop if lumbar rounding or uncontrolled twisting appears.
5. Match both sides over the week if asymmetry is a concern.

## Coaching Cues

- "Heavy but controlled"
- "Long stretch"
- "Spine stays neutral"
- "Earn the reps—do not flop"

## Common Mistakes

- Sacrificing spinal position for load
- Uncontrolled twisting
- Partial stretch every rep
- Ego loading without warm-up sets

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Deep stretch to elbow-back finish; maintain quality under fatigue.

## Progressions

- Heavier dumbbells
- Higher quality-rep targets
- Less strap reliance if grip is a goal

## Regressions

- Standard moderated DB row
- Chest-supported row
- Cable single-arm row

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets per side

### Reps

- 10–20+ challenging reps

### Intensity

- High effort; RIR ~0–2 on final sets

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1x weekly per side emphasis for most

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

High-stimulus unilateral overload method when recovered.

#### Strength

Builds grip and pulling work capacity.

#### Endurance

Inherently endurance-biased within strength work.

#### Fat Loss

Demanding; manage recovery carefully.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Not ideal for beginners or those with uncontrolled back pain.
