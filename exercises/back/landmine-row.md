# Landmine Row

## Overview

The landmine row uses an angled barbell path for bilateral or unilateral horizontal pulling. The fixed arc can feel stable and shoulder-friendly while loading the lats and mid-back.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Core and hinge stabilizers
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Barbell + landmine
- Optional T-handle or dual D-handles
- Plates

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Load the landmine bar; hinge to a stable torso angle.
2. Grip handles; arms long.
3. Row elbows back toward the torso.
4. Lower under control to stretch.
5. Keep spine neutral throughout.

## Coaching Cues

- "Hinge and brace"
- "Elbows to hips"
- "Control the arc"
- "No standing up each rep"

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive hip drive
- Rounding the back
- Partial ROM
- Jerky bar path

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Long-arm stretch to torso contraction along the landmine arc.

## Progressions

- Heavier load
- Meadows row unilateral emphasis
- Pauses

## Regressions

- Seated cable row
- Chest-supported row
- Dumbbell row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Stable free-weight row option.

#### Strength

Useful overload tool.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Good compound when bars/cables are limited.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
