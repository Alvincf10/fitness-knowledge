# Dumbbell Pullover

## Overview

The dumbbell pullover is performed lying across or along a bench while moving a dumbbell through a large shoulder-extension arc. It loads the latissimus dorsi (and can involve the chest depending on technique), providing a stretch-biased upper-body accessory.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Pectoralis major (technique-dependent)
- Triceps long head
- Serratus anterior (stabilizing role)

### Stabilizers

- Core against lumbar extension
- Shoulder stabilizers
- Glutes if bridging across the bench

## Equipment

- Dumbbell
- Flat bench
- Optional: lie along the bench for more support

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Lie along or perpendicular to a bench holding a dumbbell over the chest with arms softly bent.
2. Lower the dumbbell overhead into a deep comfortable stretch while keeping ribs from flaring excessively.
3. Pull the dumbbell back over the chest using the lats (and pecs as involved).
4. Maintain a consistent soft elbow angle.
5. Use conservative ROM if shoulders feel pinched.

## Coaching Cues

- "Soft elbows fixed"
- "Stretch without dumping the ribs"
- "Pull with the armpits/lats"
- "Do not turn it into a skull crusher"

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive lumbar extension
- Too much elbow bend (skull-crusher pattern)
- Painful end-range forcing
- Unstable dumbbell grip

## Breathing

Inhale into the stretch; exhale as you pull back over the chest.

## Range of Motion

Deep overhead stretch within shoulder comfort to dumbbell-over-chest finish.

## Progressions

- Slightly heavier load with control
- Cable pullover for continuous tension
- Tempo stretches

## Regressions

- Straight-arm pulldown
- Machine pullover
- Shorter ROM pullover

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 2–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Stretch-biased lat (and sometimes pec) accessory.

#### Strength

Not primary.

#### Endurance

Light higher reps possible.

#### Fat Loss

Optional accessory.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Avoid aggressive end ranges with shoulder irritation.
