# Feet-Elevated Inverted Row

## Overview

The feet-elevated inverted row increases the difficulty of the bodyweight horizontal pull by raising the feet, shifting more load through the upper body and increasing demand on the lats and upper back.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension
- Glutes
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Bar or rings
- Stable bench/box for feet
- Bodyweight

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set hands on the bar; place heels on an elevation so the body is nearer horizontal or decline.
2. Maintain a rigid plank.
3. Pull the chest to the bar; lower to full extension.
4. Avoid piking to cheat.
5. Progress elevation gradually.

## Coaching Cues

- "Heels up, body tight"
- "Chest to bar"
- "No pike"
- "Full stretch at the bottom"

## Common Mistakes

- Elevation too high too soon
- Hip sag/pike
- Partial depth
- Unstable foot surface

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full extension to chest-to-bar.

## Progressions

- Higher elevation
- Weighted vest
- Ring feet-elevated rows

## Regressions

- Standard inverted row
- Higher bar angle
- Cable row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 5–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Harder bodyweight horizontal-pull progression.

#### Strength

Relative strength builder.

#### Endurance

Use lower elevation for higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Effective when equipment is limited.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
