# Weighted Pull-Up

## Overview

The weighted pull-up adds external load (belt, vest, or dumbbell) to the standard pull-up to continue progressive overload after bodyweight repetitions become too easy for strength or hypertrophy goals.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Biceps and other elbow flexors
- Rhomboids and trapezius
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core musculature
- Scapular depressors/retractors
- Grip musculature

## Equipment

- Pull-up bar
- Dip/pull-up belt with plates, weight vest, or carefully held dumbbell
- Optional chalk

## Difficulty

advanced

## Execution

1. Secure external load safely (belt preferred for heavy loading).
2. Perform a strict pull-up from dead hang to chin-over-bar.
3. Lower under control to full hang.
4. Maintain scapular packing under the added load.
5. Remove load safely after the set.

## Coaching Cues

- "Load secure before you pull"
- "Same strict standards as bodyweight"
- "Full hang, full chin clearance"
- "Control the eccentric"

## Common Mistakes

- Adding load before bodyweight technique is consistent
- Kipping to move heavier loads
- Incomplete ROM
- Unsafe load attachment

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full hang to chin-over-bar; prioritize ROM quality over load.

## Progressions

- Heavier external load
- Paused weighted reps
- Slow eccentrics
- Chest-to-bar weighted pull-ups

## Regressions

- Bodyweight pull-up
- Eccentric-only weighted or bodyweight negatives
- Lat pulldown heavy

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–5 sets

### Reps

- Strength: 3–6
- Hypertrophy: 5–10

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 2–5 minutes

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly for most due to systemic fatigue

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Primary tool to keep pull-ups progressive for muscle growth.

#### Strength

Excellent absolute pulling strength builder.

#### Endurance

Not first-line; use lighter bodyweight/assisted variations.

#### Fat Loss

Keep if recovery allows; reduce load before cutting ROM.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
