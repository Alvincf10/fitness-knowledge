# Assisted Pull-Up

## Overview

The assisted pull-up reduces effective bodyweight via a machine counterweight or band so trainees can practice full-ROM vertical pulling with proper scapular mechanics before unassisted pull-ups are possible.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Biceps and elbow flexors
- Rhomboids and trapezius
- Rear deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Assisted pull-up/dip machine or heavy resistance band over a bar
- Pull-up bar

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Select assistance level that allows controlled full-ROM reps.
2. Grip the bar (pronated, neutral, or supinated as programmed).
3. Pull from a hang position to chin-over-bar with packed shoulders.
4. Lower under control.
5. Reduce assistance over weeks as capacity improves.

## Coaching Cues

- "Assistance allows quality, not laziness"
- "Full hang to chin clearance"
- "Pack the shoulders"
- "Lower slowly"

## Common Mistakes

- Too much assistance (no challenge)
- Too little assistance causing failed ugly reps
- Partial ROM
- Relying on bounce/kip

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Controlled hang to chin-over-bar.

## Progressions

- Less machine assistance or lighter band
- Unassisted pull-up
- Slow eccentrics with less help

## Regressions

- Higher assistance
- Lat pulldown
- Inverted row
- Scapular pull-up + negatives

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–3x weekly for beginners building pull-up capacity

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Allows productive vertical-pull volume before unassisted strength exists.

#### Strength

Skill and strength bridge to unassisted pull-ups.

#### Endurance

Easy to accumulate quality reps.

#### Fat Loss

Useful pulling option when fatigue is high.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
