# Yates Row

## Overview

The Yates row is an underhand (supinated) bent-over barbell row typically performed with a more upright torso angle than a near-parallel Pendlay-style row, emphasizing lat thickness and elbow-flexor involvement.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Elbow flexors
- Rhomboids and trapezius
- Rear deltoids
- Spinal erectors (isometric)

### Stabilizers

- Core hinge bracing
- Grip
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Optional straps
- Clear platform

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Hinge to a moderate torso angle (often ~45° rather than fully parallel).
2. Take a supinated grip roughly shoulder-width.
3. Row the bar to the lower abdomen by driving elbows back.
4. Lower under control to a stretch without losing brace.
5. Keep the torso angle consistent.

## Coaching Cues

- "Underhand, elbows to hips"
- "Moderate hinge—not upright curling"
- "Brace the trunk"
- "Control the eccentric"

## Common Mistakes

- Becoming a reverse curl with no back engagement
- Rounding the lumbar spine
- Excessive upright torso turning it into a shrug
- Jerky reps

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Arms long to bar-to-abdomen contraction within a stable hinge.

## Progressions

- Load
- Pause reps
- Stricter torso angles

## Regressions

- Reverse-grip seated cable row
- Chest-supported underhand machine row
- Dumbbell row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Lat-biased free-weight row with strong elbow-flexor contribution.

#### Strength

Secondary row strength.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Optional if elbows tolerate underhand loading.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Supinated gripping may aggravate elbow flexor tendons.
