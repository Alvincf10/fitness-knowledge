# Machine Row

## Overview

The machine row is a guided horizontal pull (plate-loaded or selectorized) that loads the lats and upper back with reduced balance demand, making it useful for beginners, high-volume hypertrophy work, and training without a spotter.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers; reduced versus free-hinge rows

## Equipment

- Plate-loaded or selectorized row machine
- Adjustable chest pad and seat as applicable

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Adjust seat/pad so handles align for a full stretch and natural elbow path.
2. Grab handles; start arms long.
3. Row elbows back and retract scapulae.
4. Return under control to stretch.
5. Avoid slamming the stack/plates.

## Coaching Cues

- "Full stretch, full squeeze"
- "Chest on pad if present"
- "Elbows back"
- "Control the return"

## Common Mistakes

- Poor seat height
- Partial ROM
- Momentum/jerks
- Shrugging finish

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Long-arm stretch to elbows-back contraction.

## Progressions

- Load
- Single-arm machine row
- Slow eccentrics

## Regressions

- Lighter load
- Seated cable row light
- Band row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 0–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Highly practical back volume tool.

#### Strength

Guided overload; less specificity than free rows.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient when energy is limited.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
