# Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown

## Overview

The wide-grip lat pulldown uses a pronated grip wider than shoulder width on a pulldown bar to emphasize latissimus dorsi loading in a vertical pull pattern. It is a scalable alternative or accessory to wide-grip pull-ups.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi

### Secondary

- Teres major
- Rear deltoids
- Biceps (less than closer grips for many lifters)

### Stabilizers

- Scapular retractors
- Core against the pad

## Equipment

- Lat pulldown station
- Long bar
- Thigh pad secured

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Secure thighs under the pad; grip the bar wide with a pronated grip.
2. Start with arms extended and a slight lean if preferred.
3. Pull the bar to the upper chest by driving elbows down.
4. Squeeze the back briefly; return under control to full stretch.
5. Avoid pulling behind the neck.

## Coaching Cues

- "Elbows down, not hands to chin only"
- "Bar to upper chest"
- "Control the stretch"
- "No behind-the-neck"

## Common Mistakes

- Behind-the-neck pulldowns
- Using too much momentum/lean
- Partial stretch at the top
- Excessive elbow flare without lat tension

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full overhead stretch to upper-chest contact without painful shoulder positions.

## Progressions

- Heavier load
- Paused contractions
- Pull-up progressions

## Regressions

- Neutral-grip pulldown
- Assisted pull-up
- Single-arm pulldown lighter

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Staple vertical-pull hypertrophy variation.

#### Strength

Supports pull-up strength when overloaded progressively.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep sets work well.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient back compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Avoid behind-the-neck variations.
