# Pull-Up

## Overview

The pull-up is a closed-chain vertical pulling exercise performed from a dead hang to chin-over-bar (or chest-to-bar in advanced variations). It heavily loads the latissimus dorsi and upper-back musculature with substantial elbow-flexor involvement. It is a foundational relative-strength movement and an effective hypertrophy tool when volume and progression are programmed deliberately.

## Primary Muscles

- Latissimus dorsi
- Upper-back synergists (teres major, rhomboids, trapezius involvement based on scapular action)

## Secondary Muscles

- Biceps brachii
- Brachialis and brachioradialis
- Rear delts
- Core stabilizers

## Equipment

- Pull-up bar or stable overhead handle
- Optional: resistance bands, assisted machine, weight belt/vest for progression

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Benefits

- High-transfer vertical pulling strength relative to bodyweight
- Effective lat and upper-back stimulus when full ROM is used
- Minimal equipment requirement
- Clear progression path via assistance, tempo, and added load

## Execution

1. Grip the bar with an overhand (pronated) grip, commonly slightly wider than shoulder width. Start from a dead hang with active shoulders (not passively shrugged into the ears).
2. Brace the trunk lightly. Initiate by depressing the scapulae, then pull the elbows down and toward the ribs.
3. Continue until the chin clears the bar without excessive cervical jutting or kipping.
4. Lower under control to full elbow extension.
5. Reset tension and repeat; avoid swinging between reps unless using an intentional kipping variation outside strict strength/hypertrophy work.

## Common Mistakes

- Partial reps that never reach a true hang or chin clearance
- Excessive kipping and momentum in strength/hypertrophy sets
- Shrugging to the ears and yanking with the arms only
- Overextending the lumbar spine to “finish” the rep
- Starting beyond current capacity and reinforcing poor ROM

## Coaching Cues

- “Pack the shoulders, then pull”
- “Elbows to the ribs”
- “Chest toward the bar”
- “Lower all the way without losing control”

## Programming

Use as a primary vertical pull when technique is solid. Beginners should prefer assisted variations or lat pulldowns until several strict reps are available. For hypertrophy, accumulate quality sets near technical failure. For strength, emphasize lower-rep sets, controlled eccentrics, and eventual weighted progressions.

## Sets

- Strength focus: 3–5 sets
- Hypertrophy focus: 3–4 sets

## Reps

- Strength focus: 3–6 strict reps
- Hypertrophy focus: 6–12 reps (use assistance or added load to stay in range)

## Tempo

- 2-0-1-0 to 3-1-1-0
- Optional paused top or bottom positions for control

## Rest Time

- Strength focus: 2–4 minutes
- Hypertrophy focus: 90–180 seconds

## Variations

- Chin-up (supinated)
- Neutral-grip pull-up
- Band-assisted pull-up
- Weighted pull-up
- Eccentric-only pull-up

## Regression

- Band-assisted pull-up
- Assisted pull-up machine
- Lat pulldown
- Foot-assisted eccentric pull-ups

## Progression

- Reduce assistance
- Add a weight belt or vest
- Slow eccentrics / paused reps
- Chest-to-bar strict pull-ups

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder pain in overhead hanging positions, recent elbow flexor or lat strain, or acute grip injury. Abrupt high-volume pull-up work can aggravate medial elbow or shoulder tissues in underprepared trainees. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.

## Science Summary

Pull-ups elicit high activation of the latissimus dorsi and elbow flexors and are supported as effective upper-body pulling exercises for strength and muscle development. Grip orientation shifts relative biceps versus lat emphasis but does not invalidate any standard variation when ROM and effort are equated. Compared with lat pulldowns, pull-ups impose greater relative-load and stability demands; both can contribute to hypertrophy when progressive overload is applied. Assistance and eccentric-focused strategies are evidence-consistent ways to build capacity before full strict sets.
