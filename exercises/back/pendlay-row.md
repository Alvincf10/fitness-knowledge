# Pendlay Row

## Overview

The Pendlay row is a strict bent-over barbell row performed from a dead stop on the floor each rep. Removing stretch-reflex contribution emphasizes explosive upper-back/lat force production and technique discipline.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors
- Spinal erectors (isometric hinge)

### Stabilizers

- Core bracing in the hinge
- Scapular stabilizers
- Grip

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Plates that allow the bar to rest at mid-shin height
- Clear floor space

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set a hip hinge with the bar over mid-foot; grip like a deadlift/row hybrid.
2. Brace; pull the bar explosively to the lower chest/upper abdomen.
3. Lower under control to a full dead stop on the floor.
4. Reset tightness every rep—do not bounce.
5. Maintain a consistent torso angle near parallel when possible.

## Coaching Cues

- "Dead stop every rep"
- "Explode to the torso"
- "Stay hinged"
- "Reset before the next pull"

## Common Mistakes

- Touch-and-go bouncing
- Standing up out of the hinge
- Rounding the back
- Pulling too high into a shrug

## Breathing

Brace and brief hold before the pull; exhale as the bar reaches the torso or after the rep.

## Range of Motion

Floor dead stop to torso contact; full reset between reps.

## Progressions

- Heavier loads
- Paused at the torso
- Deficit Pendlay if mobility allows

## Regressions

- Chest-supported row
- Seal row
- Lighter conventional barbell row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–5 sets

### Reps

- Strength: 3–6
- Hypertrophy: 5–8

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 2–4 minutes

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Useful for strict, high-tension row volume.

#### Strength

Excellent explosive row strength builder.

#### Endurance

Not ideal.

#### Fat Loss

Use lighter technique work if fatigued.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
