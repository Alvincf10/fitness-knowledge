# Seal Row

## Overview

The seal row is a chest-supported free-weight row performed lying prone on a raised bench so the weights hang freely. It minimizes momentum and lumbar involvement while heavily loading the back musculature.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Latissimus dorsi
- Rhomboids and middle trapezius

### Secondary

- Rear deltoids
- Elbow flexors

### Stabilizers

- Scapular stabilizers
- Minimal hip/lumbar contribution

## Equipment

- Sturdy elevated bench or seal-row station
- Barbell or dumbbells
- Plates that can pass under the bench

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Lie prone on an elevated bench; grip barbell or dumbbells hanging below.
2. Row the load toward the bench/torso by driving elbows up/back.
3. Squeeze scapulae at the top.
4. Lower to a full stretch without bouncing plates off the floor (adjust bench height).
5. Keep thighs/chest supported throughout.

## Coaching Cues

- "Body stays quiet on the bench"
- "Elbows to the ceiling"
- "Stretch at the bottom"
- "No hip drive"

## Common Mistakes

- Bench too low causing floor bang every rep
- Using hip thrust to move weight
- Partial ROM
- Jerky reps

## Breathing

Inhale to prepare or during the eccentric lengthening phase. Exhale during the concentric pull (or near peak contraction). Brace the trunk; avoid breath-holding that causes dizziness on high-rep sets.

## Range of Motion

Full hang stretch to elbows-back contraction.

## Progressions

- Heavier load
- Paused seal rows
- Tempo eccentrics

## Regressions

- Chest-supported machine row
- Incline dumbbell row with torso on pad
- Seated cable row

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

High-tension back builder with strict form.

#### Strength

Useful strict row strength.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Great when you want back stimulus without hinge fatigue.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic shoulder impingement, acute elbow tendinopathy, recent lat or biceps strain, uncontrolled low-back pain during hinged positions, or inability to control scapular motion. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
