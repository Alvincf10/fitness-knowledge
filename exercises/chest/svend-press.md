# Svend Press

## Overview

The Svend press is a low-load chest isolation/activation drill performed by pressing plates together while extending the arms forward, emphasizing pectoralis major isometric adduction with a pressing component.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids
- Triceps (minor)

### Stabilizers

- Scapular musculature
- Core in standing variations

## Equipment

- One or more weight plates pressed together
- Optional: standing or seated posture

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Press two plates (or a single plate with palms) together hard at mid-chest.
2. Maintain maximal inward squeeze.
3. Extend arms forward slowly while continuing the squeeze.
4. Return to the chest under squeeze tension.
5. Keep shoulders from elevating.

## Coaching Cues

- "Crush the plates together"
- "Arms extend, squeeze never stops"
- "Do not shrug"
- "Slow and deliberate"

## Common Mistakes

- Losing the squeeze and turning into a front raise
- Using too much load
- Shrugging
- Rushing reps

## Breathing

Steady breathing; exhale gently on extension.

## Range of Motion

From chest to near-full forward reach while squeeze is maintained.

## Progressions

- Slightly heavier plates
- Longer pauses at extension
- Cable squeeze press variations

## Regressions

- Isometric plate squeeze without press
- Pec deck light

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–3 sets

### Reps

- 10–20

### Intensity

- RIR 2–4; tension-focused

### Rest

- 45–75 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly as activator/finisher

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Finisher/activation; not a primary mass builder.

#### Strength

Not for strength.

#### Endurance

Compatible with higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Optional low-stress accessory.

## Contraindications

Stop if shoulder or wrist pain appears. Evidence for hypertrophy stimulus is limited versus loaded presses/flies. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
