# Machine Chest Press

## Overview

The machine chest press is a guided horizontal press that loads the pectoralis major with triceps and anterior deltoid contribution. Fixed paths reduce stability demand versus free weights, supporting beginners, higher-rep hypertrophy work, and training without a spotter.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Reduced versus free weights; residual scapular and trunk bracing still matter

## Equipment

- Plate-loaded or selectorized chest press machine
- Adjust seat and handles per machine design

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Adjust seat height so handles align roughly with mid-chest.
2. Grip handles; set scapulae against the pad without shrugging.
3. Press to near-full elbow extension without slamming the stack.
4. Return under control to a comfortable stretch position.
5. Keep pressure even between sides.

## Coaching Cues

- "Handles at mid-chest"
- "Press through the palms evenly"
- "Control the return into the stretch"
- "Ribs down; no shrugging"

## Common Mistakes

- Poor seat height altering shoulder stress
- Unintentional partial ROM
- Slamming the stack
- Losing pad contact and shrugging
- Twisting to one side

## Breathing

Exhale during the press; inhale during the return.

## Range of Motion

Use available machine ROM that allows a chest stretch without painful positions.

## Progressions

- Increase load
- Slow eccentrics
- Single-arm machine press if available
- Progress toward dumbbell or barbell press

## Regressions

- Lighter load with higher control
- Push-up regressions
- Landmine press

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- Hypertrophy: 8–15
- Endurance: 12–20

### Intensity

- RIR 0–3 depending on proximity-to-failure goals

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds (up to 150 s for heavy plate-loaded sets)

### Frequency

- 1–3 sessions weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Practical for adding chest volume with lower skill demand.

#### Strength

Limited free-weight 1RM specificity; useful guided overload tool.

#### Endurance

Well suited to higher-rep sets.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient compound option when energy is limited.

## Contraindications

Avoid if the machine path forces painful shoulder positions; adjust seat or choose another press. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
