# Decline Dumbbell Fly

## Overview

The decline dumbbell fly performs the fly pattern on a declined bench, often increasing relative lower-chest stretch and sternal pectoralis emphasis as an isolation accessory.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (sternal emphasis)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Rotator cuff
- Scapular retractors

## Equipment

- Light dumbbells
- Decline bench with secure leg supports

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Secure legs on a decline bench; hold dumbbells over the chest with soft elbows.
2. Open into a controlled stretch arc.
3. Keep elbow angle fixed.
4. Return by adducting without pressing.
5. Use conservative loads due to stretch stress.

## Coaching Cues

- "Legs secure"
- "Soft elbows"
- "Stretch under control"
- "Fly, do not press"

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive load
- Unsafe decline setup
- Painful overstretch
- Momentum

## Breathing

Inhale into stretch; exhale on return.

## Range of Motion

Pain-free deep stretch to finish over lower/mid chest.

## Progressions

- Tempo
- High cable fly pairing

## Regressions

- Flat dumbbell fly lighter
- Pec deck
- High cable fly

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–3 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 2–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Lower-chest isolation accessory.

#### Strength

Not for strength.

#### Endurance

Light higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Optional accessory.

## Contraindications

Higher stretch stress; avoid with anterior shoulder irritation. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
