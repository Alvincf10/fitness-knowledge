# Weighted Push-Up

## Overview

The weighted push-up adds external load via vest, plate, or backpack to progress the classic push-up after bodyweight reps become too easy for hypertrophy or strength goals.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Trunk musculature

## Equipment

- Weight vest or securely placed plate
- Flat floor
- Spotter optional for plate loading

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Secure external load safely.
2. Assume a rigid plank.
3. Perform full-ROM push-ups with controlled tempo.
4. Keep alignment under the added load.
5. Remove load safely after the set.

## Coaching Cues

- "Load is secure before you start"
- "Same plank standards as bodyweight"
- "Full depth"
- "Quality reps over ego load"

## Common Mistakes

- Unsecured plates sliding
- Sagging under load
- Cutting depth
- Excessive load causing form breakdown

## Breathing

Inhale down; exhale up.

## Range of Motion

Chest to near-floor through controlled depth.

## Progressions

- Heavier vest
- Deficit weighted push-up
- Paused weighted reps

## Regressions

- Bodyweight push-up
- Deficit without load
- Incline push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 5–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Allows progressive overload when high-rep push-ups stall.

#### Strength

Effective relative-strength tool.

#### Endurance

Use lighter loads for higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Excellent minimal-equipment compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
