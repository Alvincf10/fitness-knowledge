# Decline Dumbbell Press

## Overview

The decline dumbbell press loads the pectoralis major on a declined bench with independent dumbbells, often emphasizing sternal fibers while allowing a natural shoulder path and deep stretch.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (sternal emphasis often increased)

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular retractors
- Rotator cuff

## Equipment

- Dumbbells
- Decline bench with secure leg supports

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Secure legs; bring dumbbells to the start position at the sides of the chest.
2. Press to elbow extension without clanging aggressively.
3. Lower under control into a stretch.
4. Maintain even depth and tempo.
5. Sit up carefully after the set.

## Coaching Cues

- "Secure legs first"
- "Lower into a controlled stretch"
- "Wrists over elbows"
- "Even lockout"

## Common Mistakes

- Insecure leg position
- Excessive bounce
- Asymmetrical ROM
- Unsafe teardown

## Breathing

Inhale during the controlled eccentric (lowering). Brace the trunk, then exhale through the concentric press or near lockout. Avoid prolonged breath-holds on high-rep sets if dizziness occurs.

## Range of Motion

Full comfortable stretch and lockout; avoid painful shoulder end ranges.

## Progressions

- Heavier loads
- Tempo eccentrics
- Pauses

## Regressions

- Machine press
- Flat dumbbell press
- Assisted dips

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Useful lower-chest biased free-weight press.

#### Strength

Accessory only.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Optional compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
