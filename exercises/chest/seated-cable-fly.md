# Seated Cable Fly

## Overview

The seated cable fly uses a supported seat or bench between dual cables to reduce lower-body cheating while isolating the pectoralis major under continuous tension.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular musculature against the bench
- Reduced core demand versus standing

## Equipment

- Dual cable station
- Bench or seat positioned between stacks
- Handles

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Place a bench between cables; sit or lie supported as designed.
2. Start arms open with soft elbows.
3. Fly handles together to midline.
4. Return under control to stretch.
5. Keep upper back stable on the support.

## Coaching Cues

- "Supported back, soft elbows"
- "Hug to the midline"
- "Control the stretch"
- "No torso swing"

## Common Mistakes

- Bench placement causing asymmetric pull
- Pressing pattern
- Overstretch pain
- Using momentum from the hips

## Breathing

Exhale on squeeze; inhale on opening.

## Range of Motion

Comfortable stretch to midline squeeze.

## Progressions

- Load
- Standing cable fly
- Single-arm seated fly

## Regressions

- Pec deck
- Lighter standing fly

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Stable isolation volume.

#### Strength

Not primary.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep friendly.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory.

## Contraindications

Reduce stretch if shoulders hurt. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
