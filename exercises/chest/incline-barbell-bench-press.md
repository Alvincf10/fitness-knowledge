# Incline Barbell Bench Press

## Overview

The incline barbell bench press is performed on an inclined bench and loads the pectoralis major with increased relative demand on the clavicular fibers and anterior deltoids compared with flat pressing. A moderate incline preserves a pressing pattern while allowing precise barbell progressive overload.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (greater relative clavicular-head emphasis at moderate inclines)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids
- Triceps brachii

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Scapular retractors
- Trunk musculature against the bench

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Incline bench (commonly ~15–45°)
- Rack or dedicated incline station
- Spotter or safety arms recommended for heavy sets

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set a moderate incline (often 15–45°). Lie with feet planted and eyes roughly under the bar.
2. Retract and depress the scapulae; grip evenly, slightly wider than shoulder width.
3. Unrack and hold the bar over the upper chest/shoulder line.
4. Lower under control to the upper-chest contact point with elbows at a moderate, comfortable angle.
5. Press to full elbow extension without losing upper-back contact, then re-rack when stable.

## Coaching Cues

- "Moderate incline, not a shoulder press"
- "Upper back tight; chest up"
- "Lower to the upper chest"
- "Drive evenly through both arms"

## Common Mistakes

- Incline set too steep (becomes a shoulder press)
- Excessive elbow flare
- Bouncing the bar
- Losing scapular support and shrugging
- Uneven bar path

## Breathing

Inhale during the controlled eccentric (lowering). Brace the trunk, then exhale through the concentric press or near lockout. Avoid prolonged breath-holds on high-rep sets if dizziness occurs.

## Range of Motion

Use full controllable ROM to the upper chest without painful shoulder positions. Avoid chronically shortened ROM unless specifically programmed.

## Progressions

- Heavier load with solid technique
- Paused incline reps
- Slow eccentrics (3–4 s)
- Lower RIR over a mesocycle

## Regressions

- Incline dumbbell press
- Incline machine chest press
- Landmine press
- Elevated-hand push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- Strength: 3–5 sets
- Hypertrophy: 3–4 sets

### Reps

- Strength: 3–6
- Hypertrophy: 6–12

### Intensity

- Strength: ~80–90% 1RM or RIR 1–3
- Hypertrophy: ~65–80% 1RM or RIR 1–3

### Rest

- Strength: 2–5 minutes
- Hypertrophy: 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3 sessions per week within total chest/push volume

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Primary or secondary upper-chest press; pair with flat pressing and fly/cable work.

#### Strength

Useful incline strength accessory; flat bench remains more specific for competition pressing.

#### Endurance

Higher-rep sets (12–20) with lighter loads if conditioning is the goal.

#### Fat Loss

Keep as a compound lift in a deficit; manage volume to protect recovery.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis. Very steep inclines may aggravate shoulders in some lifters.
