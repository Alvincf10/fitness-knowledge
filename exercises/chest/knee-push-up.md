# Knee Push-Up

## Overview

The knee push-up is a regression of the standard push-up that shortens the lever, reducing load while teaching the horizontal press pattern and trunk control for beginners.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Trunk musculature (reduced lever versus full plank)

## Equipment

- Bodyweight
- Soft surface optional under knees

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Place hands under/near shoulders; knees on the floor; keep a straight line from knees to head.
2. Lower the chest toward the floor.
3. Press to full elbow extension.
4. Avoid hinging at the hips as a substitute for arm bend.
5. Progress to full push-ups when form is consistent.

## Coaching Cues

- "Knees to head in one line"
- "Chest down, not hips first"
- "Full lockout"
- "Quality before quantity"

## Common Mistakes

- Piking hips
- Partial depth
- Hands too far forward
- Collapsing shoulders

## Breathing

Inhale down; exhale up.

## Range of Motion

Chest approaches floor with controlled scapular motion.

## Progressions

- Standard push-up
- Incline-to-floor progression
- Tempo knee push-ups

## Regressions

- Incline push-up (hands elevated)
- Wall push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–4x weekly for beginners

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Entry hypertrophy stimulus when progressed toward harder variations.

#### Strength

Beginner strength foundation.

#### Endurance

Easy to accumulate reps.

#### Fat Loss

Accessible conditioning/press work.

## Contraindications

Wrist/shoulder pain may require elevation or handles. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
