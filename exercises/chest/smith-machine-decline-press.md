# Smith Machine Decline Press

## Overview

The Smith machine decline press combines a declined torso with a rails-guided bar path, often emphasizing sternal pectoralis fibers while providing safeties for guided overload.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (sternal emphasis often increased)

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular retractors; reduced free-bar stability demand

## Equipment

- Smith machine
- Decline bench aligned to lower/mid-chest touch
- Safety stops

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Secure legs on the decline bench under the Smith bar; align for lower/mid-chest contact.
2. Set safeties. Unhook the bar.
3. Lower under control to the chest.
4. Press to extension without bouncing.
5. Re-hook fully before exiting.

## Coaching Cues

- "Secure legs and safeties"
- "Lower-chest touch"
- "No bounce"
- "Re-hook completely"

## Common Mistakes

- Insecure decline setup
- Misaligned bench path
- Ignoring safeties
- Excessive bounce

## Breathing

Inhale on descent; exhale on press.

## Range of Motion

Touch lower/mid chest if comfortable; shorten if rails force painful angles.

## Progressions

- Load increases
- Pause reps
- Free-weight decline progressions when ready

## Regressions

- Decline dumbbell press
- Machine chest press
- High cable fly + flat press pairing

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Guided lower-chest biased overload.

#### Strength

Limited free-bar specificity.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Optional compound when setup is safe.

## Contraindications

Avoid if decline position causes dizziness or shoulder pain. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
