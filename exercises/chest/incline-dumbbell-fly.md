# Incline Dumbbell Fly

## Overview

The incline dumbbell fly performs the fly pattern on a moderate incline, increasing relative upper-chest (clavicular) emphasis while providing a stretch-biased isolation stimulus.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (clavicular emphasis)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Rotator cuff
- Scapular retractors

## Equipment

- Dumbbells
- Incline bench (~15–45°)

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set a moderate incline; hold dumbbells over the upper chest with soft elbows.
2. Open in an arc into a controlled stretch.
3. Keep elbow angle fixed.
4. Return by adducting without pressing.
5. Maintain upper-back contact.

## Coaching Cues

- "Moderate incline"
- "Stretch the upper chest"
- "Soft elbows"
- "Fly, do not press"

## Common Mistakes

- Too-steep incline
- Excessive load
- Overstretch pain
- Converting to incline press

## Breathing

Inhale on opening; exhale on closing.

## Range of Motion

Pain-free stretch to finish over upper chest.

## Progressions

- Load/tempo
- Low cable fly pairing

## Regressions

- Pec deck
- Light low cable fly
- Flat dumbbell fly lighter

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 2–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Upper-chest isolation accessory.

#### Strength

Not primary.

#### Endurance

Light higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory.

## Contraindications

Avoid painful end ranges; reduce incline or ROM. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
