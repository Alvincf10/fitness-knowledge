# Dumbbell Fly

## Overview

The dumbbell fly is an isolation exercise emphasizing pectoralis major lengthening and adduction on a flat bench. Resistance is highest near the stretch and decreases toward the top, so control and moderate loads matter more than heavy loading.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Rotator cuff
- Scapular retractors against the bench

## Equipment

- Light-to-moderate dumbbells
- Flat bench

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Lie on a flat bench holding dumbbells over the chest with soft elbows.
2. Open the arms in a wide arc until a deep comfortable stretch is reached.
3. Keep elbow angle relatively fixed.
4. Reverse the arc by adducting until dumbbells return over the chest.
5. Avoid turning the movement into a press.

## Coaching Cues

- "Hug a wide barrel"
- "Soft elbows stay soft"
- "Stretch under control"
- "Do not press the weight up"

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive load forcing a press pattern
- Dangerous overstretch
- Bent elbows changing every rep
- Bouncing out of the bottom

## Breathing

Inhale into the stretch; exhale as arms return.

## Range of Motion

Deep stretch without anterior shoulder pain; finish over mid-chest.

## Progressions

- Slightly heavier loads with control
- Slow eccentrics
- Incline dumbbell fly
- Cable fly for continuous tension

## Regressions

- Pec deck
- Cable fly light
- Shorter ROM fly

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 2–3 (leave technique buffer)

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Classic stretch-biased chest isolation.

#### Strength

Not for strength.

#### Endurance

Light higher-rep sets possible.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory only.

## Contraindications

Avoid aggressive end-range stretches with shoulder irritation. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
