# Landmine Press

## Overview

The landmine press uses a barbell anchored in a landmine attachment, creating an angled pressing path that is often shoulder-friendly. It trains the pectoralis major and anterior deltoids with high stability demands in standing or half-kneeling setups.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (clavicular fibers often involved due to the press angle)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids (substantial contribution)
- Triceps brachii

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-rotation musculature
- Scapular stabilizers
- Glutes/trunk in standing variations

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Landmine attachment or secure corner setup
- Optional weight plates

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Load the landmine bar. Hold the end at shoulder height (half-kneeling or standing).
2. Brace the trunk; keep ribs down.
3. Press the bar forward and up along the landmine arc to extension.
4. Lower under control to the shoulder.
5. Complete all reps on one side before switching, or alternate as programmed.

## Coaching Cues

- "Brace before you press"
- "Punch along the bar path"
- "Do not flare the ribs"
- "Finish tall without shrugging"

## Common Mistakes

- Lumbar hyperextension
- Turning into a pure shoulder press with no trunk brace
- Incomplete lockout control
- Rotating the torso excessively

## Breathing

Exhale on the press; inhale on the return. Brace anti-rotation.

## Range of Motion

From shoulder height to full reach along the bar arc without pain.

## Progressions

- Heavier load
- Standing instead of kneeling
- Single-arm tempo work

## Regressions

- Two-hand landmine press
- Machine press
- Incline push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–12 per side

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Useful angled press and upper-chest/shoulder-friendly option.

#### Strength

Good unilateral pressing strength tool.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient compound when time-limited.

## Contraindications

Avoid if pressing arc provokes shoulder pain; modify stance or choose machine press. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
