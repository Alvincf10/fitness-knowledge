# Pec Deck

## Overview

The pec deck (machine fly) isolates the pectoralis major on a fixed arc with back support. Reduced cheating via torso momentum makes it accessible for controlled hypertrophy work.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Minimal versus standing cables; scapular positioning against the pad still matters

## Equipment

- Pec deck / machine fly
- Adjustable seat and arm pads or handles

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Set seat height so elbows/forearms align near mid-chest.
2. Place forearms on pads (or grip handles) with a soft elbow bend.
3. Bring arms together through horizontal adduction toward midline.
4. Squeeze briefly; return under control to a comfortable stretch.
5. Avoid slamming the stack.

## Coaching Cues

- "Seat height so elbows are level"
- "Hug inward, do not press"
- "Control the opening stretch"
- "Ribs down on the pad"

## Common Mistakes

- Seat height causing impingement angles
- Pressing instead of flying
- Bouncing out of the stretch with excessive load
- Neck jutting forward
- Uncontrolled eccentrics

## Breathing

Exhale as arms close; inhale as arms open.

## Range of Motion

Open only within a pain-free stretch; close to near full adduction.

## Progressions

- Load increases
- Tempo eccentrics
- Peak-contraction pauses

## Regressions

- Lighter load
- Partial pain-free arc
- Light cable fly

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3 times weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Reliable isolation volume with easy progressive overload.

#### Strength

Not a strength priority lift.

#### Endurance

Effective for higher-rep sets.

#### Fat Loss

Optional accessory after presses.

## Contraindications

Stop deep stretch if it causes sharp shoulder pain; adjust seat or choose cables. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
