# Decline Barbell Bench Press

## Overview

The decline barbell bench press uses a declined torso position, often increasing relative emphasis on the sternal (lower) pectoralis fibers while reducing some shoulder flexion demand versus steep incline work. It is a compound press for hypertrophy and strength accessory programming when setup is secure.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (sternal/lower fibers often relatively emphasized)

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular retractors
- Trunk musculature secured by decline pads

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Decline bench with secure leg supports
- Rack or partner hand-off when needed

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Secure legs under the decline pads and set a stable upper-back position.
2. Take an even grip; unrack carefully (partner hand-off is often safer).
3. Hold the bar over the lower-to-mid chest line.
4. Lower under control to the chest with wrists stacked over elbows.
5. Press to lockout and re-rack with assistance if required.

## Coaching Cues

- "Secure the legs before unracking"
- "Lower to the lower chest"
- "Control the eccentric; do not bounce"
- "Press evenly to lockout"

## Common Mistakes

- Unstable leg securement
- Excessive bounce
- Aggressive elbow flare
- Unsafe unracking/racking
- Extreme decline creating instability

## Breathing

Inhale during the controlled eccentric (lowering). Brace the trunk, then exhale through the concentric press or near lockout. Avoid prolonged breath-holds on high-rep sets if dizziness occurs.

## Range of Motion

Touch or approach the lower/mid chest with a controlled path. Do not sacrifice shoulder comfort for extreme depth.

## Progressions

- Progressive overload
- Pause reps
- Tempo eccentrics

## Regressions

- Decline dumbbell press
- Flat dumbbell press
- Machine chest press
- Assisted chest dip

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- Hypertrophy: 6–12
- Strength accessory: 4–8

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3 for most working sets

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds (up to 3 minutes for heavy sets)

### Frequency

- 1–2 sessions weekly as part of total press volume

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Useful for lower-chest emphasis within a balanced pressing menu.

#### Strength

Accessory for most lifters; flat bench remains primary for powerlifting specificity.

#### Endurance

Prefer push-ups or machine press for high-rep endurance work.

#### Fat Loss

Keep if technique and secure setup remain safe when fatigued.

## Contraindications

Avoid if decline positioning causes dizziness, uncontrolled shoulder pain, or if the bench cannot be secured safely. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
