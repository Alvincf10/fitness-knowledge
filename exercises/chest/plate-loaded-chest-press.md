# Plate-Loaded Chest Press

## Overview

The plate-loaded chest press (e.g., iso-lateral hammer-style machines) allows independent arm loading with a guided path, combining some free-weight feel with machine stability for chest hypertrophy and strength work.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular musculature; less than free weights

## Equipment

- Plate-loaded chest press machine
- Weight plates
- Adjustable seat

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Adjust seat so handles meet mid-chest.
2. Load plates evenly (or unevenly for unilateral emphasis).
3. Press to extension without slamming.
4. Return to a controlled stretch.
5. Keep shoulders packed against the pad.

## Coaching Cues

- "Seat first, then load"
- "Even or intentional uneven loading"
- "Control the stretch"
- "No shrug"

## Common Mistakes

- Poor seat height
- Slamming plates
- Asymmetry without intent
- Cutting ROM

## Breathing

Exhale on press; inhale on return.

## Range of Motion

Stretch to near lockout within comfort.

## Progressions

- More plates
- Slow eccentrics
- Single-arm emphasis

## Regressions

- Selectorized machine press
- Push-up
- Landmine press

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–150 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent machine hypertrophy tool.

#### Strength

Useful overload without a spotter.

#### Endurance

Moderate-high reps workable.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient compound.

## Contraindications

Adjust path/seat if shoulders hurt. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
