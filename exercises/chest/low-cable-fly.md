# Low Cable Fly

## Overview

The low cable fly uses low pulleys and a low-to-high arc, often increasing relative emphasis on the clavicular (upper) pectoralis fibers. It pairs well with incline pressing in hypertrophy programs.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (clavicular/upper fibers relatively emphasized)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids
- Biceps (minor stabilizing role)

### Stabilizers

- Core and scapular stabilizers
- Rotator cuff

## Equipment

- Dual low cable pulleys
- Handles

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set pulleys low; grab handles and step forward into tension.
2. Begin with hands low and out, elbows softly bent.
3. Raise and adduct in an arc, finishing near upper-chest level without shrugging.
4. Squeeze briefly.
5. Lower under control to the start stretch.

## Coaching Cues

- "Low to high"
- "Reach up and in"
- "Soft elbows locked in angle"
- "Do not shrug the finish"

## Common Mistakes

- Finish becomes a pure front raise
- Hip drive/momentum
- Trap shrugging
- Elbow angle collapsing into a curl
- Lumbar overarching

## Breathing

Exhale during the upward squeeze; inhale on the eccentric return.

## Range of Motion

Finish high enough for upper-chest contraction without pain; open low within joint-friendly limits.

## Progressions

- Load, pauses, slow eccentrics
- Single-arm low-to-high fly

## Regressions

- Light incline dumbbell fly
- Mid cable fly
- Pec deck

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3 times weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Strong accessory pairing with incline presses.

#### Strength

Not primary.

#### Endurance

Good for higher-rep finishers.

#### Fat Loss

Use as accessory volume only.

## Contraindications

Avoid if low-to-high arcs provoke shoulder discomfort; shorten ROM. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
