# Ring Push-Up

## Overview

The ring push-up is an unstable closed-chain horizontal press on gymnastic rings. Instability increases demand on the chest, triceps, anterior deltoids, serratus, and trunk versus floor push-ups, making it an advanced progression when volume is controlled.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids
- Serratus anterior

### Stabilizers

- Rotator cuff
- Abdominals and trunk musculature
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Gymnastic rings set just above the floor
- Stable anchor
- Optional parallettes as an intermediate step

## Difficulty

advanced

## Execution

1. Set rings low and even; assume a plank grip on the rings.
2. Create tension by slightly externally rotating/"breaking" the rings.
3. Lower the chest between the rings while keeping a rigid plank.
4. Descend only as deep as you can stabilize.
5. Press to full extension without losing ring control.

## Coaching Cues

- "Break the rings apart"
- "Body rigid like a plank"
- "Chest through, not hips sagging"
- "Smooth press, rings quiet"

## Common Mistakes

- Uneven ring height
- Hip sag or pike
- Collapsing into the shoulders
- Excessive depth beyond stability
- Uncontrolled elbow flare

## Breathing

Inhale on the way down; exhale on the press. Brace throughout.

## Range of Motion

Deepest stable ROM; do not chase depth at the expense of shoulder centering.

## Progressions

- More instability (lower rings/longer straps)
- Feet-elevated ring push-up
- Weighted vest
- Ring dip pathway

## Regressions

- Floor push-up
- Deficit push-up on stable handles
- Incline ring push-up
- Standard incline push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 4–10 controlled reps

### Intensity

- RIR 2–4 early; progress toward RIR 1–2 as skill allows

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3 sessions weekly depending on shoulder recovery

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Effective when sets are stable; pair with stable presses if instability limits stimulus.

#### Strength

Builds pressing stability and relative strength.

#### Endurance

Not ideal until technique is consistent.

#### Fat Loss

Use only if form holds under fatigue.

## Contraindications

Inappropriate without solid floor push-up technique, or with symptomatic shoulder instability/pain. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
