# Wide Push-Up

## Overview

The wide push-up places the hands outside typical shoulder width to increase relative pectoralis major demand versus a standard push-up, with somewhat reduced triceps contribution for many trainees.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids
- Triceps brachii

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Trunk musculature

## Equipment

- Bodyweight
- Flat floor

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Place hands wider than shoulder width.
2. Assume a rigid plank.
3. Lower the chest toward the floor with controlled elbow flare within comfort.
4. Press to extension.
5. Keep hips aligned.

## Coaching Cues

- "Hands wide, body tight"
- "Chest to floor"
- "Control the elbows"
- "Full lockout"

## Common Mistakes

- Hands so wide shoulders feel pinched
- Hip sag
- Partial depth
- Neck leading

## Breathing

Inhale down; exhale up.

## Range of Motion

Chest approaches floor through controllable depth.

## Progressions

- Deficit wide push-up
- Weighted vest
- Feet-elevated wide push-up

## Regressions

- Standard push-up
- Incline wide push-up
- Knee push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–20

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Simple chest-biased bodyweight variation.

#### Strength

Limited absolute overload.

#### Endurance

Good higher-rep option.

#### Fat Loss

Useful minimal-equipment work.

## Contraindications

Reduce width if shoulders feel irritated. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
