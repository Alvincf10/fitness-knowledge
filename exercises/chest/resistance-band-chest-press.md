# Resistance Band Chest Press

## Overview

The resistance band chest press is a portable horizontal press using elastic resistance. Accommodating resistance increases load toward lockout and provides a home-training or warm-up option for the pectoralis major, triceps, and anterior deltoids.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension musculature in standing variations
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Resistance band(s)
- Stable anchor at mid-chest height (or band around the back for press-outs)
- Handles optional

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Anchor the band at roughly mid-chest height, or loop a band around the upper back for press-outs.
2. Hold handles at the sides of the chest; step out to create tension.
3. Press forward to extension without shrugging.
4. Return under control, keeping tension on the band.
5. Match left/right press paths.

## Coaching Cues

- "Create tension before the first press"
- "Press through mid-chest line"
- "Control the return"
- "Do not let the band snap you back"

## Common Mistakes

- Slack at the start of the rep
- Excessive lumbar extension
- Uneven arm paths
- Anchor failure / unsafe setup

## Breathing

Exhale on press; inhale on return.

## Range of Motion

From chest-level start to near-full elbow extension with continuous band tension.

## Progressions

- Heavier/thicker bands
- Slow eccentrics
- Single-arm band press
- Transition to dumbbell/cable press

## Regressions

- Lighter band
- Wall push-up
- Incline push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–20

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 45–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Convenient hypertrophy stimulus when gym equipment is limited.

#### Strength

Limited maximal strength overload versus free weights.

#### Endurance

Excellent higher-rep option.

#### Fat Loss

Useful minimal-equipment compound.

## Contraindications

Ensure anchors are secure. Stop if shoulder pain appears. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
