# Single-Arm Dumbbell Bench Press

## Overview

The single-arm dumbbell bench press loads one side at a time, increasing anti-rotation core demand and allowing unilateral strength/hypertrophy work to address side-to-side asymmetries.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-rotation musculature
- Scapular stabilizers
- Opposite-side trunk bracing

## Equipment

- One dumbbell
- Flat bench
- Free hand for bracing or light assistance as needed

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Lie on a flat bench with one dumbbell at the working shoulder.
2. Brace hard against rotation; non-working hand can hold the bench.
3. Press to lockout without twisting the torso off the bench.
4. Lower under control.
5. Complete prescribed reps, then switch sides.

## Coaching Cues

- "Brace like someone is pushing your ribs"
- "Hips and shoulders stay square"
- "Press without rotating"
- "Match depth side to side"

## Common Mistakes

- Allowing torso rotation
- Unequal ROM between sides
- Oversized load causing instability
- Shoulder dumping at the bottom

## Breathing

Exhale on press; inhale on lowering; maintain brace.

## Range of Motion

Same deep comfortable stretch as a bilateral dumbbell press.

## Progressions

- Heavier load
- Tempo eccentrics
- Pause reps

## Regressions

- Bilateral dumbbell press
- Machine single-arm press
- Landmine press

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets per side

### Reps

- 8–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds between sides/sets

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Unilateral hypertrophy and asymmetry work.

#### Strength

Unilateral pressing strength.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Time-efficient if rest is managed.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
