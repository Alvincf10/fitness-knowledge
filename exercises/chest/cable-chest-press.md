# Cable Chest Press

## Overview

The cable chest press is a standing or seated horizontal press using dual cables, providing continuous tension and a customizable path for pectoralis major loading with elevated core demand when standing.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension/anti-rotation
- Scapular stabilizers

## Equipment

- Dual cable station at roughly mid-chest height
- Handles

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set cables near mid-chest; hold handles at the sides of the chest and step forward into a split stance.
2. Brace the trunk.
3. Press handles forward to extension without shrugging.
4. Return under control to the chest.
5. Keep elbows tracking in a moderate path.

## Coaching Cues

- "Split stance, brace hard"
- "Press through mid-chest line"
- "Control the return"
- "Do not let cables pull you back"

## Common Mistakes

- Staggered stance too narrow causing loss of balance
- Turning into a fly
- Excessive lumbar extension
- Partial unfinished presses

## Breathing

Exhale on press; inhale on return.

## Range of Motion

From chest-level start to near-full elbow extension.

## Progressions

- Load
- Single-arm cable press
- Slow eccentrics

## Regressions

- Machine chest press
- Seated cable press if available
- Push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Continuous-tension compound accessory.

#### Strength

Secondary strength tool.

#### Endurance

Good higher-rep option.

#### Fat Loss

Useful when free weights are limited.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
