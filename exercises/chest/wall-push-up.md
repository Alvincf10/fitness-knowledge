# Wall Push-Up

## Overview

The wall push-up is the lightest common push-up regression, performed standing with hands on a wall. It teaches the horizontal press pattern with minimal load and is appropriate for absolute beginners or early return-to-pressing progressions.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Trunk musculature (upright)

## Equipment

- Bodyweight
- Clear wall space

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Stand facing a wall; place hands on the wall at roughly chest height and shoulder width.
2. Walk feet back to create a mild lean while keeping a straight body line.
3. Bend elbows to bring the chest toward the wall.
4. Press back to full elbow extension.
5. Progress by stepping feet farther back or moving to an incline push-up.

## Coaching Cues

- "Body in one line"
- "Chest to wall"
- "Full lockout"
- "Step farther back to progress"

## Common Mistakes

- Feet too close (almost no load)
- Sagging through the hips
- Partial range
- Hands far above head turning into a shoulder-dominant pattern

## Breathing

Inhale toward the wall; exhale as you press away.

## Range of Motion

Chest approaches the wall each rep; elbows extend fully on the press.

## Progressions

- Feet farther from the wall
- Incline push-up on a counter/bench
- Knee push-up
- Floor push-up

## Regressions

- Closer feet
- Smaller ROM if needed for comfort

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 45–75 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–4x weekly for beginners

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Entry hypertrophy stimulus when progressed.

#### Strength

Beginner pressing foundation.

#### Endurance

Easy higher-rep practice.

#### Fat Loss

Accessible low-stress pressing.

## Contraindications

Stop if wrist or shoulder pain appears; adjust hand height. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
