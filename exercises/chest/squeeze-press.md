# Squeeze Press

## Overview

The squeeze press (hex/crush press) is a dumbbell press variation where the bells are pressed together throughout the ROM, increasing isometric adduction demand on the pectoralis major while still training the press pattern.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular retractors
- Rotator cuff

## Equipment

- Pair of dumbbells
- Flat (or incline) bench

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Lie on a bench holding dumbbells together over the chest (hex ends touching if hexagonal).
2. Squeeze the dumbbells inward hard.
3. Press up while maintaining the squeeze.
4. Lower under control with continued inward pressure.
5. Do not let the bells separate.

## Coaching Cues

- "Crush the bells together"
- "Press and squeeze"
- "Control the descent"
- "Keep contact between bells"

## Common Mistakes

- Losing inward pressure
- Excessive load that prevents squeeze
- Flaring and separating bells
- Partial unfinished lockouts

## Breathing

Inhale during the controlled eccentric (lowering). Brace the trunk, then exhale through the concentric press or near lockout. Avoid prolonged breath-holds on high-rep sets if dizziness occurs.

## Range of Motion

Full press ROM while dumbbells remain in contact.

## Progressions

- Heavier dumbbells with maintained squeeze
- Incline squeeze press
- Tempo work

## Regressions

- Svend press
- Machine press
- Light dumbbell press

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Useful press variation emphasizing pec adduction.

#### Strength

Secondary.

#### Endurance

Moderate.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
