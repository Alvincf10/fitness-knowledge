# Deficit Push-Up

## Overview

The deficit push-up elevates the hands so the chest can descend below hand level. Increased ROM provides a larger stretch stimulus to the pectoralis major while retaining closed-chain push-up mechanics.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Trunk musculature in plank position

## Equipment

- Bodyweight
- Parallettes, sturdy handles, or stable equal-height plates

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Place hands on equal-height elevated surfaces near shoulder width.
2. Assume a rigid plank.
3. Lower the chest below hand level through a controlled stretch.
4. Press to full elbow extension.
5. Maintain alignment; do not sag to buy depth.

## Coaching Cues

- "Chest below the hands"
- "Plank stays tight"
- "Soft stretch, not a dump into the shoulders"
- "Push the handles away"

## Common Mistakes

- Deficit too large for comfort
- Hip sagging
- Unstable hand surfaces
- Uncontrolled elbow flare
- Bouncing out of the bottom

## Breathing

Inhale into the bottom stretch; exhale on the ascent.

## Range of Motion

Deep but pain-free stretch. Reduce deficit height if shoulders complain.

## Progressions

- Greater deficit (gradual)
- Weighted vest
- Slow eccentrics / stretch pauses
- Ring push-up

## Regressions

- Standard push-up
- Incline push-up
- Smaller deficit

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds (up to 150 s if weighted)

### Frequency

- 1–3 times weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Strong bodyweight option emphasizing stretch-mediated stimulus.

#### Strength

Good relative-strength builder when loaded.

#### Endurance

Use moderate deficits for higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Effective when standard push-up capacity is already solid.

## Contraindications

Avoid large deficits with anterior shoulder pain or poor scapular control at depth. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
