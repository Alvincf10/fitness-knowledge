# Close-Grip Push-Up

## Overview

The close-grip push-up narrows hand placement to increase triceps demand while still loading the pectoralis major. It is a bodyweight pressing variation for triceps-biased chest training and lockout strength.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major
- Triceps brachii (elevated relative demand versus a standard push-up)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Trunk musculature

## Equipment

- Bodyweight
- Flat floor

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Place hands closer than shoulder width (diamond optional if wrists allow).
2. Assume a rigid plank.
3. Lower the chest toward the hands with elbows tracking nearer the torso.
4. Press to full extension.
5. Keep hips aligned.

## Coaching Cues

- "Hands narrow, elbows nearer ribs"
- "Plank stays tight"
- "Chest toward hands"
- "Full lockout"

## Common Mistakes

- Hands so narrow wrists painfully collapse
- Hip sag
- Flaring despite close grip
- Cutting depth

## Breathing

Inhale down; exhale up.

## Range of Motion

Chest approaches hands through controllable depth.

## Progressions

- Deficit close-grip push-up
- Weighted vest
- Ring close-grip push-up

## Regressions

- Incline close-grip push-up
- Knee variation
- Standard push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Triceps-biased chest accessory.

#### Strength

Helps lockout pressing strength.

#### Endurance

Good higher-rep option.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient bodyweight finisher.

## Contraindications

Wrist pain may require handles or a wider "close" grip. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
