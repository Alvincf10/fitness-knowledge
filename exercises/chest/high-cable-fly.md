# High Cable Fly

## Overview

The high cable fly uses high pulleys and a high-to-low arc, often increasing relative emphasis on the sternal/lower pectoralis fibers while maintaining continuous cable tension through adduction.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (sternal/lower fibers relatively emphasized)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids
- Serratus anterior (depending on scapular motion)

### Stabilizers

- Core anti-extension/anti-rotation musculature
- Rotator cuff

## Equipment

- Dual high cable pulleys
- Handles

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set both pulleys high; grasp handles and step forward to create tension.
2. Start with arms elevated and open, elbows softly bent.
3. Sweep handles downward and inward toward the lower chest/upper abdomen line.
4. Pause briefly with the pectoralis major contracted.
5. Return slowly without changing elbow angle.

## Coaching Cues

- "High to low"
- "Lead with the elbows in an arc"
- "Finish low at the midline"
- "No torso fold"

## Common Mistakes

- Triceps pushdown pattern
- Excessive trunk flexion
- Locked/strained elbows
- Overstretching at the top
- Jerky return

## Breathing

Exhale on the downward squeeze; inhale on the return.

## Range of Motion

Smooth arc from a comfortable overhead-open position to a low midline finish; stop if the shoulder feels pinched.

## Progressions

- Load, tempo, or peak pause
- Single-arm high-to-low fly

## Regressions

- Mid cable fly
- Pec deck
- Decline-oriented press accessories

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 10–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3 times weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Accessory for lower-chest emphasis and continuous tension.

#### Strength

Not primary.

#### Endurance

Suitable for higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Accessory after compounds.

## Contraindications

Reduce ROM or avoid if overhead-open positions irritate the shoulder. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
