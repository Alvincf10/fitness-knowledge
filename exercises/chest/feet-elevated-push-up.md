# Feet-Elevated Push-Up

## Overview

The feet-elevated push-up raises the feet to increase load through the upper body and shift demand somewhat toward the upper chest and shoulders relative to a floor push-up.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids
- Triceps brachii

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Trunk musculature

## Equipment

- Bodyweight
- Stable box/bench for feet

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Place feet on a stable elevation; hands on the floor under shoulders.
2. Maintain a rigid plank line.
3. Lower the chest toward the floor.
4. Press to full extension.
5. Avoid piking or sagging.

## Coaching Cues

- "Body in one line"
- "Chest to floor"
- "Do not pike"
- "Push the floor away"

## Common Mistakes

- Elevation too high too soon
- Hip pike
- Unstable foot surface
- Partial depth

## Breathing

Inhale down; exhale up.

## Range of Motion

Chest approaches floor with controlled scapular motion.

## Progressions

- Higher elevation
- Weighted vest
- Ring feet-elevated push-up

## Regressions

- Floor push-up
- Lower elevation
- Incline push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 5–12

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Harder bodyweight progression for chest loading.

#### Strength

Relative strength builder.

#### Endurance

Use lower elevation for higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Good when equipment is limited.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
