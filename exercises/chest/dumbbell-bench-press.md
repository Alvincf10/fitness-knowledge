# Dumbbell Bench Press

## Overview

The dumbbell bench press is a flat-bench horizontal press using independent dumbbells. It loads the pectoralis major, triceps, and anterior deltoids while allowing a natural wrist/shoulder path and, for many lifters, a deeper stretch ROM than a barbell.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Rotator cuff (dynamic stability)
- Scapular retractors

## Equipment

- Pair of dumbbells
- Flat bench

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Sit with dumbbells on thighs; lie back while bringing the dumbbells to the sides of the chest.
2. Plant feet; lightly retract scapulae against the bench.
3. Press until elbows extend; avoid forcefully clanging the bells.
4. Lower under control to a deep but comfortable stretch near chest level.
5. After the set, return dumbbells to the thighs and sit up safely.

## Coaching Cues

- "Wrists stacked over elbows"
- "Lower into a controlled stretch"
- "Press up without losing back contact"
- "Soft lockout, not a crash together"

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive elbow flare
- Chronically short ROM
- Hips lifting off the bench
- Unsafe setup/teardown
- Dumbbells drifting forward of the shoulder line

## Breathing

Inhale during the controlled eccentric (lowering). Brace the trunk, then exhale through the concentric press or near lockout. Avoid prolonged breath-holds on high-rep sets if dizziness occurs.

## Range of Motion

Seek a full stretch at the bottom without painful anterior shoulder positions. Prioritize left-right symmetry.

## Progressions

- Heavier dumbbells
- Slow eccentrics
- Pauses in the stretch
- Single-arm dumbbell bench press

## Regressions

- Machine chest press
- Floor press
- Push-up
- Neutral-grip dumbbell press with lighter load

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- Hypertrophy: 8–12
- Strength-biased: 5–8

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 90–180 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3 times per week within weekly pressing volume

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Excellent primary or secondary flat press due to ROM and independent loading.

#### Strength

Strong accessory; load jumps are less precise than small barbell plates.

#### Endurance

Higher reps are feasible with lighter dumbbells.

#### Fat Loss

Retain as a compound lift; reduce sets before sacrificing technique.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
