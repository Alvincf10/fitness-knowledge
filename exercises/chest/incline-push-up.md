# Incline Push-Up

## Overview

The incline push-up elevates the hands to reduce load versus a floor push-up, serving as a primary regression and a joint-friendly horizontal press for beginners or high-rep accessory work.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Serratus anterior
- Trunk musculature

## Equipment

- Bodyweight
- Stable bench, box, or bar for hand elevation

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Place hands on a stable elevated surface; walk feet back into a plank.
2. Lower the chest toward the surface.
3. Press to full extension.
4. Keep a rigid body line.
5. Lower the elevation over time as strength improves.

## Coaching Cues

- "Hands on a solid surface"
- "Body straight"
- "Chest to the edge"
- "Full lockout"

## Common Mistakes

- Unstable elevation surface
- Hip sag/pike
- Partial depth
- Elevation so high stimulus disappears

## Breathing

Inhale down; exhale up.

## Range of Motion

Chest approaches the elevated surface each rep.

## Progressions

- Lower hand elevation
- Floor push-up
- Deficit push-up

## Regressions

- Higher incline
- Wall push-up
- Knee push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 2–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 60–90 seconds

### Frequency

- 2–4x weekly for beginners

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Scalable hypertrophy entry point.

#### Strength

Beginner pressing strength.

#### Endurance

Excellent higher-rep option.

#### Fat Loss

Accessible compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
