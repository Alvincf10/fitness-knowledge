# Reverse-Grip Bench Press

## Overview

The reverse-grip bench press uses a supinated grip on the barbell, which can increase upper-chest and triceps emphasis for some lifters. It requires careful setup and lower loads than a pronated bench due to grip security and unfamiliar groove.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (clavicular fibers may be relatively emphasized)

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Forearm flexors for grip
- Scapular retractors

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Flat bench and rack
- Spotter strongly recommended
- Optional thumb wraps / secure grip practices

## Difficulty

advanced

## Execution

1. Set up as in a flat bench press but take a supinated (reverse) grip with thumbs around the bar.
2. Unrack carefully with a spotter when possible.
3. Lower under control to the chest with elbows tracking appropriately.
4. Press to lockout without losing grip security.
5. Re-rack with assistance.

## Coaching Cues

- "Thumbs around the bar"
- "Spotter for unrack and rack"
- "Controlled groove"
- "Do not chase pronated bench loads"

## Common Mistakes

- Thumbless grip (high risk)
- Excessive load early
- No spotter on heavy attempts
- Elbow positions that stress wrists/shoulders

## Breathing

Inhale during the controlled eccentric (lowering). Brace the trunk, then exhale through the concentric press or near lockout. Avoid prolonged breath-holds on high-rep sets if dizziness occurs.

## Range of Motion

Full controllable touch and press; prioritize grip security over depth ego.

## Progressions

- Gradual load increases only after groove mastery
- Pause reps light

## Regressions

- Incline dumbbell press
- Close-grip push-up
- Standard barbell bench

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 6–10

### Intensity

- Conservative RIR 2–4 until proficient

### Rest

- 2–3 minutes

### Frequency

- 1x weekly for most

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Optional variety for upper-chest/triceps emphasis.

#### Strength

Not first-line for most strength goals.

#### Endurance

Poor choice due to grip risk when fatigued.

#### Fat Loss

Prefer safer presses when fatigued.

## Contraindications

High technical/grip risk. Avoid with wrist issues or without a spotter. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
