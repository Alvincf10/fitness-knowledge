# Pause Barbell Bench Press

## Overview

The pause barbell bench press inserts a deliberate pause on the chest, removing stretch-reflex contribution and increasing control demands. It is a strength and hypertrophy variation of the flat barbell bench press used to improve technique and pressing power off the chest.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular retractors
- Trunk and leg drive musculature

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Flat bench and rack
- Spotter/safeties recommended

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Set up as in the barbell bench press.
2. Lower under control to the chest.
3. Pause motionless on the chest for the prescribed time (commonly 1 second).
4. Press explosively to lockout without losing positions.
5. Re-rack safely.

## Coaching Cues

- "Settle on the chest, still"
- "No bounce"
- "Drive after the pause"
- "Keep upper back tight during the pause"

## Common Mistakes

- Soft bounce disguised as a pause
- Losing tightness during the pause
- Flaring under fatigue
- Inconsistent pause lengths

## Breathing

Inhale on descent/brace; brief hold during pause if appropriate; exhale on press.

## Range of Motion

Full touch with a true motionless pause; lockout each rep.

## Progressions

- Longer pauses
- Heavier paused work
- Chains/bands only if already proficient

## Regressions

- Touch-and-go bench with lighter load
- Floor press
- Dumbbell press

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–5 sets

### Reps

- Strength: 2–5
- Hypertrophy: 5–8

### Intensity

- Often slightly below touch-and-go loads; RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 2–5 minutes

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Improves control and time under tension off the chest.

#### Strength

Highly specific for paused pressing strength.

#### Endurance

Not ideal.

#### Fat Loss

Use lighter paused technique work if fatigued.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
