# Incline Machine Chest Press

## Overview

The incline machine chest press provides guided upper-horizontal pressing with elevated relative clavicular pectoralis and anterior deltoid demand, with lower skill requirements than free-weight incline presses.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major (clavicular emphasis)

### Secondary

- Anterior deltoids
- Triceps brachii

### Stabilizers

- Reduced versus free weights; maintain scapular contact with pad

## Equipment

- Incline chest press machine
- Adjustable seat

## Difficulty

beginner

## Execution

1. Set seat so handles align with upper chest.
2. Press to near-full extension without slamming.
3. Return to a controlled stretch.
4. Keep ribs down and shoulders from shrugging.
5. Match left/right effort.

## Coaching Cues

- "Upper-chest handle line"
- "Control the stretch"
- "Even press"
- "No shrug"

## Common Mistakes

- Seat too high/low
- Partial ROM without intent
- Stack slamming
- Turning into a shrug-dominant pattern

## Breathing

Exhale on press; inhale on return.

## Range of Motion

Pain-free stretch to near lockout.

## Progressions

- Load
- Tempo
- Transition to incline dumbbell press

## Regressions

- Flat machine press
- Landmine press
- Incline push-up

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–4 sets

### Reps

- 8–15

### Intensity

- RIR 0–3

### Rest

- 60–120 seconds

### Frequency

- 1–3x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Easy upper-chest volume tool.

#### Strength

Guided overload only.

#### Endurance

Good for higher reps.

#### Fat Loss

Efficient accessory/compound hybrid.

## Contraindications

Adjust or avoid if path irritates shoulders. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
