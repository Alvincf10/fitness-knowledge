# Floor Press

## Overview

The floor press is a horizontal press performed lying on the floor, which shortens ROM by stopping when the triceps meet the floor. It reduces shoulder extension demand and emphasizes mid-range pressing strength for the chest and triceps.

## Muscles Worked

### Primary

- Pectoralis major

### Secondary

- Triceps brachii
- Anterior deltoids

### Stabilizers

- Scapular retractors
- Trunk against the floor

## Equipment

- Barbell or dumbbells
- Flat floor space
- Optional rack for barbell floor press

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Execution

1. Lie on the floor under the bar (or with dumbbells). Plant feet or extend legs based on preference.
2. Unrack and hold over the chest.
3. Lower until upper arms lightly contact the floor.
4. Pause briefly to eliminate bounce.
5. Press to lockout.

## Coaching Cues

- "Touch the floor softly with the arms"
- "Pause, then press"
- "Keep upper back tight"
- "No bounce off the floor"

## Common Mistakes

- Bouncing elbows off the floor
- Excessive flare
- Losing bar path over the chest
- Rushing the pause

## Breathing

Inhale during the controlled eccentric (lowering). Brace the trunk, then exhale through the concentric press or near lockout. Avoid prolonged breath-holds on high-rep sets if dizziness occurs.

## Range of Motion

From lockout down to upper-arm floor contact; intentionally partial versus full bench ROM.

## Progressions

- Heavier paused reps
- Close-grip floor press
- Transition to full ROM bench

## Regressions

- Machine press
- Push-up
- Lighter dumbbell floor press

## Programming

### Sets

- 3–5 sets

### Reps

- Strength: 3–6
- Hypertrophy: 6–10

### Intensity

- RIR 1–3

### Rest

- 2–4 minutes for heavy work

### Frequency

- 1–2x weekly

### Best Goals

#### Hypertrophy

Useful overload with less shoulder stress for some lifters.

#### Strength

Excellent mid-range lockout strength builder.

#### Endurance

Not first-line.

#### Fat Loss

Optional compound.

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic anterior shoulder irritation, recent pectoralis or triceps strain, uncontrolled wrist/elbow pain, or when a safe setup/spot cannot be arranged. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.
