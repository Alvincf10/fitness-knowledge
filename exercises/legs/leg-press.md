# Leg Press

## Overview

The leg press is a machine-based multi-joint lower-body exercise that loads the quadriceps and glutes with reduced balance and axial spinal loading compared with barbell back squats. It is commonly used for hypertrophy volume, beginners learning pressing patterns, and trainees who need a squat alternative with more constrained mechanics.

## Primary Muscles

- Quadriceps

## Secondary Muscles

- Gluteus maximus
- Hamstrings (supporting role dependent on foot placement and depth)
- Adductors

## Equipment

- Leg press machine (45-degree sled or seated horizontal variants)

## Difficulty

beginner

## Benefits

- Accessible progressive overload for lower-body hypertrophy
- Lower balance demand than free-weight squats
- Useful for adding quad/glute volume without additional barbell axial load
- Foot-placement options can bias comfort and regional emphasis

## Execution

1. Sit in the machine with the low back and hips firmly against the pad. Place feet on the platform at roughly shoulder width (adjust for comfort and goal).
2. Unlock the safeties. Lower the platform under control by bending the knees and hips.
3. Descend to a depth that keeps the pelvis from tucking aggressively and the low back from rounding off the pad.
4. Press through the midfoot to extend the knees and hips without forcefully locking out aggressively if that irritates the joints.
5. Re-engage safeties after the set.

## Common Mistakes

- Allowing the pelvis to posteriorly tilt / low back to round at the bottom
- Excessively deep ROM that sacrifices pelvic control for load
- Partial reps that never approach a meaningful knee flexion stimulus
- Letting knees collapse inward
- Placing feet so high/low that hips or knees become painfully leveraged without intent

## Coaching Cues

- “Tailbone and low back stay glued to the pad”
- “Control the descent”
- “Push the platform away through midfoot”
- “Knees track over toes”

## Programming

Use as a primary machine squat pattern for beginners or as secondary hypertrophy volume after squats/hinges. It is especially useful for high-quality quad volume when free-weight fatigue is already high. Manage depth by pelvic control, not ego ROM.

## Sets

- 3–4 sets

## Reps

- 8–15 reps (hypertrophy default)
- 6–8 reps for heavier strength-biased machine work

## Tempo

- 2-0-1-0 to 3-1-1-0

## Rest Time

- 90–180 seconds

## Variations

- High-foot leg press
- Low-foot / more quad-biased placement
- Single-leg leg press
- Narrow or wider stance variants

## Regression

- Reduced ROM to pain-free controlled depth
- Goblet squat
- Seated machine squat / hack-style alternatives if available

## Progression

- Progressive loading
- Slower eccentrics
- Controlled deeper ROM with pelvic stability
- Single-leg overload

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic knee pain aggravated by deep loaded flexion, uncontrolled lumbar flexion on the sled, or recent lower-body injury. Avoid bouncing out of the bottom. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.

## Science Summary

Machine leg presses produce substantial quadriceps activation and can increase lower-body strength and muscle size when progressive overload is applied. Compared with back squats, the leg press generally reduces balance demand and barbell axial loading while still providing a meaningful multi-joint stimulus. Foot position can alter hip versus knee moments and comfort, but hypertrophy still depends mainly on tension, volume, and effort. Pelvic control at depth is a practical limiter: once lumbar rounding appears, additional ROM is usually counterproductive.
