# Back Squat

## Overview

The back squat is a barbell squat variation with the load supported across the upper back. It is one of the most researched lower-body compound exercises for developing quadriceps, glute, and overall lower-body strength and hypertrophy, with substantial trunk stabilization demand.

## Primary Muscles

- Quadriceps
- Gluteus maximus

## Secondary Muscles

- Hamstrings (synergists/stabilizers)
- Adductors
- Erector spinae
- Abdominals and other trunk stabilizers
- Calves (supporting role)

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Squat rack or power rack
- Safety pins recommended for heavy sets

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Benefits

- High systemic loading for lower-body strength development
- Effective quadriceps and glute stimulus when depth and progressive overload are managed
- Trains coordinated multi-joint lower-body force production
- Strong evidence base across strength and hypertrophy literature

## Execution

1. Set the bar on a rack at upper-chest height. Step under and place it on the upper traps (high-bar) or rear delts/shelf (low-bar). Create a firm upper-back shelf.
2. Unrack, step back, and set feet roughly shoulder-width with toes slightly turned out as needed for hip anatomy.
3. Brace the trunk. Sit the hips down and back while knees track over the toes.
4. Descend to at least roughly parallel thigh depth when mobility and comfort allow, keeping heels planted.
5. Drive upward to stand while maintaining balance over midfoot; re-rack under control.

## Common Mistakes

- Excessive forward torso collapse from poor brace or ankle/hip constraints
- Knees collapsing inward under load
- Cutting depth inconsistently without a programming reason
- Heels rising and shifting onto the toes
- Losing upper-back tightness and letting the bar roll

## Coaching Cues

- “Big breath, ribs down, brace”
- “Spread the floor”
- “Knees over toes, tracking in line”
- “Chest proud, stand tall”

## Programming

Use as a primary lower-body strength or hypertrophy lift. Strength blocks favor lower reps and longer rests. Hypertrophy blocks can use moderate loads, controlled eccentrics, and accessory volume from presses or hinges. Individualize stance and bar position to anthropometry and comfort.

## Sets

- Strength focus: 3–5 sets
- Hypertrophy focus: 3–4 sets

## Reps

- Strength focus: 3–6 reps
- Hypertrophy focus: 6–12 reps

## Tempo

- Strength: controlled eccentric, intentional concentric (often 2-0-X-0)
- Hypertrophy: 2-0-1-0 to 3-1-1-0

## Rest Time

- Strength focus: 2–5 minutes
- Hypertrophy focus: 2–3 minutes

## Variations

- High-bar back squat
- Low-bar back squat
- Pause squat
- Tempo squat
- Safety-bar squat

## Regression

- Goblet squat
- Leg press
- Box squat to a controlled target height

## Progression

- Progressive overload via load/reps
- Pause squats
- Tempo eccentrics
- Greater consistent depth within safe ROM

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate with symptomatic acute knee or lumbar pain aggravated by axial loading, recent lower-body injury, or inability to maintain balance/brace under the bar. Use rack safeties for heavy work. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.

## Science Summary

Back squats are strongly supported for increasing lower-body strength and contributing to muscle hypertrophy when progressive resistance training guidelines are followed. EMG and training studies show substantial quadriceps and glute involvement, with bar position and stance altering joint moments and regional emphasis. Parallel or deeper squats can increase hypertrophic stimulus for the glutes and adductors in many trainees when technique is solid. Weekly volume, proximity to failure, and recovery capacity determine outcomes more than any single squat style.
