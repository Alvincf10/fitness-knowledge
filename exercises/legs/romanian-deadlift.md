# Romanian Deadlift

## Overview

The Romanian deadlift (RDL) is a hip-hinge exercise that emphasizes the hamstrings and glutes while training isometric trunk stability. Unlike a conventional deadlift from the floor, the RDL typically starts from standing and lowers the load through a controlled hip flexion pattern with relatively limited knee bend, making it a primary posterior-chain hypertrophy and strength accessory.

## Primary Muscles

- Hamstrings
- Gluteus maximus

## Secondary Muscles

- Erector spinae (isometric support)
- Adductors (synergistic support depending on stance)
- Forearms / grip musculature
- Upper-back stabilizers

## Equipment

- Barbell
- Optional: dumbbells or trap bar as variations
- Optional: lifting straps when grip limits posterior-chain loading

## Difficulty

intermediate

## Benefits

- Targeted posterior-chain loading with measurable progressive overload
- Useful for hamstring and glute hypertrophy when ROM and tension are controlled
- Reinforces hip-hinge mechanics relevant to deadlifting and athletic tasks
- Can be programmed with less absolute load than floor deadlifts for many trainees

## Execution

1. Stand tall holding the barbell at hip level with a shoulder-width stance and slight knee bend.
2. Brace the trunk. Unlock the hips and push them backward while keeping the bar close to the legs.
3. Lower until a strong hamstring stretch is reached without losing a neutral spine (depth is individual).
4. Drive the hips forward to return to standing, finishing with glute contraction without hyperextending the lumbar spine.
5. Reset breath/brace and repeat.

## Common Mistakes

- Rounding the lumbar spine to chase depth
- Turning the movement into a squat by excessive knee bend
- Letting the bar drift away from the legs
- Jerking the lockout with lumbar hyperextension
- Cutting the eccentric short and removing the stretch stimulus

## Coaching Cues

- “Hips back, bar close”
- “Soft knees, not a squat”
- “Long spine to the stretch”
- “Stand tall by driving the hips through”

## Programming

Use as a primary hinge accessory on lower or posterior-chain days. Hypertrophy sets usually work best in moderate reps with slow eccentrics. Keep technical quality high; when low-back fatigue degrades form, reduce load or substitute a supported hinge variation.

## Sets

- Strength focus: 3–4 sets
- Hypertrophy focus: 3–4 sets

## Reps

- Strength focus: 4–6 reps
- Hypertrophy focus: 6–12 reps

## Tempo

- 2-0-1-0 to 3-1-1-0

## Rest Time

- Strength focus: 2–3 minutes
- Hypertrophy focus: 90–180 seconds

## Variations

- Dumbbell Romanian deadlift
- Single-leg Romanian deadlift
- Deficit Romanian deadlift
- Tempo RDL

## Regression

- Hip hinge with dowel feedback
- Cable pull-through
- Elevated short-ROM RDL with lighter load

## Progression

- Progressive loading
- Slower eccentrics
- Deeper controlled ROM
- Pause in the stretch position

## Contraindications

May be inappropriate during symptomatic low-back pain aggravated by hinging, recent hamstring strain, or uncontrolled discogenic symptoms. Depth and load should be conservative when technique is inconsistent. This is training guidance, not medical diagnosis.

## Science Summary

Hip-hinge patterns load the hamstrings and gluteus maximus effectively and are standard in resistance-training programs for posterior-chain strength and hypertrophy. The RDL’s longer eccentric emphasis and stretch position make it particularly useful for hamstring development when progressive overload is applied. Spinal erectors work largely isometrically to maintain torso position; poor bracing shifts stress to passive spinal tissues. As with other free-weight hinges, weekly volume and proximity to technical failure matter more than minor stance variations.
