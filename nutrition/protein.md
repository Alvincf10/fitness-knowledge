# Protein

## Overview

Protein provides essential amino acids required to repair and build muscle tissue. For resistance trainees, total daily protein intake is one of the strongest nutrition levers for hypertrophy and for retaining lean mass during fat loss.

## Definition

Dietary protein is made of amino acids. Complete proteins provide all essential amino acids in useful amounts. Muscle protein synthesis (MPS) rises when protein feedings supply enough leucine and essential amino acids within an overall adequate daily intake.

## Daily Recommendation

- Hypertrophy / serious resistance training: about 1.6–2.2 g/kg body mass/day for most trainees
- During aggressive deficits, intakes toward the higher end can help spare lean mass
- Distribute across roughly 3–5 feedings when practical (often ~0.3–0.55 g/kg per meal)

## Benefits

- Supports muscle hypertrophy with progressive resistance training
- Improves lean-mass retention while cutting
- Increases satiety relative to equal calories from some other macros
- Supports recovery from hard training

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming whey alone builds muscle without progressive training
- Ignoring total daily intake while obsessing over the post-workout shake
- Using BCAAs instead of complete protein when totals are low
- Setting targets too low during a calorie deficit

## Example Foods

- Chicken, turkey, lean beef, fish, eggs
- Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, milk
- Whey or casein protein powder
- Tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans paired for amino acid coverage

## Practical Tips

- Hit the daily range first; then refine meal distribution
- Use a shake only when food access is limited
- Track intake for 1–2 weeks if progress stalls
- Raise protein before extreme cardio increases when cutting

## Evidence Summary

Meta-analyses show higher protein intakes augment strength and fat-free mass gains from resistance training, especially when baseline intake is suboptimal. ISSN and ACSM guidance support elevated protein for exercising populations beyond sedentary RDAs. Distribution helps, but daily total remains primary.
