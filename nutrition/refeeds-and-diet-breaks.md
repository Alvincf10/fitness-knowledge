# Refeeds and Diet Breaks

## Overview

Refeeds and diet breaks are planned pauses or temporary calorie increases during a fat-loss phase. They are used to support adherence, training performance, and psychological sustainability—not to “reset metabolism” overnight.

## Definition

A refeed is a short period (often 1 day) of higher carbohydrate/calorie intake. A diet break is a longer return toward maintenance (often 1–2 weeks) before resuming a deficit.

## Daily Recommendation

- Keep protein high during refeeds; raise carbs primarily
- Use diet breaks after prolonged deficits or when fatigue and adherence worsen
- Track that weekly average intake still supports the intended fat-loss timeline
- Do not treat every hungry day as an unplanned binge refeed

## Benefits

- Can improve training performance and glycogen during hard cuts
- May improve adherence and reduce diet fatigue
- Provides structured relief versus chaotic cheat days
- Helps some athletes sustain longer fat-loss campaigns

## Common Mistakes

- Uncontrolled cheat days that erase the weekly deficit
- Expecting a single refeed to reverse all adaptive thermogenesis
- Taking diet breaks so often that fat loss never accumulates
- Dropping protein during higher-calorie days

## Example Foods

- Higher-carb refeed meals: rice, potatoes, fruit, pasta with lean protein
- Maintenance-style mixed meals during diet breaks
- Familiar foods that satisfy without complete loss of structure

## Practical Tips

- Schedule refeeds around hardest training days
- Define calorie targets for refeed/break days in advance
- Evaluate progress across multi-week blocks including breaks
- Return to the planned deficit without guilt after the break

## Evidence Summary

Emerging research on intermittent diet breaks suggests possible adherence and fat-loss efficiency benefits versus continuous deficits in some contexts, though results are mixed and implementation varies. Physiological adaptation is real but not magically cured by one high-carb day. Evidence grade is moderate.
