# Pre-Workout Nutrition

## Overview

Pre-workout nutrition fuels the upcoming session and helps avoid hunger or GI distress during training. For most lifters, a digestible mix of carbohydrate and protein eaten in a practical window beats complicated stacks.

## Definition

Pre-workout nutrition is food or fluid consumed in the hours before training to support energy availability, comfort, and performance. It is distinct from stimulant supplements, though they are often combined.

## Daily Recommendation

- If the last meal was 3–4+ hours earlier, eat a small-to-moderate carb-forward meal or snack 1–3 hours pre-training
- Include some protein when it sits well
- Keep fat and fiber moderate immediately pre-session if GI-sensitive
- Hydrate ahead of hard or hot sessions

## Benefits

- Supports high-intensity performance and training volume
- Reduces risk of underfueled sessions that cut effective weekly stimulus
- Improves consistency for early or after-work workouts

## Common Mistakes

- Large fatty meals right before hard lifting
- Relying only on stimulants while glycogen and hydration are poor
- Copying endurance gel strategies for short easy sessions
- Skipping food when performance is clearly suffering

## Example Foods

- Banana and yogurt
- Toast with eggs or turkey
- Oats with milk protein
- Rice and lean meat earlier in the pre-workout window

## Practical Tips

- Trial foods in training before using them on important days
- Early-morning lifters can use a small liquid or easy snack if a full meal is unrealistic
- Align caffeine timing with personal sleep tolerance
- Keep a repeatable default meal for busy days

## Evidence Summary

ISSN nutrient-timing and ACSM sports-nutrition guidance support carbohydrate availability before demanding exercise. Protein pre-exercise contributes to daily targets and can support MPS; exact minute timing is less critical than arriving fueled.
