# Maintenance

## Overview

Maintenance is the energy intake level that keeps body weight and body composition relatively stable over time. It is used between growth and fat-loss phases to consolidate results and restore training quality.

## Definition

Energy maintenance equals average expenditure over a representative period, so fat mass and lean mass remain roughly unchanged when training and lifestyle stay consistent.

## Daily Recommendation

- Estimate from recent intake that held weight steady for 2+ weeks
- Keep protein elevated if muscle retention/growth quality remains a priority
- Match carbohydrate to training load while holding weight stable
- Recalculate after large weight changes or training shifts

## Benefits

- Stabilizes hormones, hunger, and recovery after aggressive cuts
- Allows skill and strength focus without surplus fat gain
- Provides a baseline for planning future bulks or cuts
- Improves long-term adherence versus perpetual dieting

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a single day’s intake as maintenance
- Jumping straight from a deep cut into a large bulk
- Letting protein collapse because “calories are maintenance”
- Ignoring step-count and NEAT changes that shift true maintenance

## Example Foods

- Balanced mixed meals similar to cutting/bulking templates with calories adjusted to stability
- Consistent protein anchors at each meal
- Flexible carb/fat adjustments around training days

## Practical Tips

- Use weekly average weight to confirm maintenance
- Keep training progressive even at maintenance for recomposition potential in intermediates
- Schedule deliberate maintenance phases after long cuts
- Adjust intake when job activity or cardio changes

## Evidence Summary

Energy balance physics underpin maintenance. Sports-nutrition practice uses maintenance phases to improve adherence and performance sustainability between physique phases. Evidence supports matching intake to expenditure for weight stability.
