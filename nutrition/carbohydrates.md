# Carbohydrates

## Overview

Carbohydrates are the primary fuel for high-intensity training and a major contributor to glycogen restoration. Intake should scale with training volume and performance goals rather than follow a single universal low-carb rule.

## Definition

Carbohydrates include sugars and starches that digest to glucose and support muscle and liver glycogen. Fiber-containing carbohydrate foods also affect satiety and cardiometabolic health.

## Daily Recommendation

- Scale to training load: higher for endurance or high-volume training, moderate for general lifting
- Many active trainees land roughly in ranges consistent with sports-nutrition guidance when performance matters (often several g/kg/day depending on sport and phase)
- Fat-loss phases can reduce carbs while keeping protein high and training quality acceptable

## Benefits

- Fuels intense and prolonged training
- Supports glycogen replenishment between sessions
- Helps sustain training volume important for hypertrophy
- Carbohydrate-rich foods can carry micronutrients and fiber

## Common Mistakes

- Cutting carbs so low that performance and weekly volume collapse
- Treating all carbohydrate as uniquely fattening outside of calorie balance
- Relying only on sugars without overall diet quality
- Ignoring peri-workout fueling needs in two-a-day or long sessions

## Example Foods

- Rice, potatoes, oats, bread, pasta
- Fruit and dairy
- Beans and lentils
- Sports drinks or gels for long sessions

## Practical Tips

- Place more carbs around hard training when performance matters
- Keep protein fixed, then adjust carbs for energy needs and body-composition phase
- Use easy-to-digest carbs before sensitive stomach workouts
- Reassess carb intake when adding cardio or training frequency

## Evidence Summary

ACSM and ISSN consensus supports carbohydrate availability for endurance and high-intensity performance. For hypertrophy, carbs mainly support training quality and total energy; they are not uniquely anabolic beyond enabling progressive overload and adequate calories.
