# Bulking

## Overview

Bulking is a planned calorie surplus combined with progressive resistance training to maximize muscle gain. A controlled surplus favors lean gains better than unstructured overeating.

## Definition

Bulking means eating above maintenance energy needs for a defined phase while prioritizing protein intake and progressive overload. “Lean bulk” implies a modest surplus; “dreamer bulk” implies a large surplus with excess fat gain.

## Daily Recommendation

- Start with a modest surplus, often about 250–500 kcal/day above maintenance for many intermediates
- Protein about 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day
- Keep carbs high enough to fuel hard training; fats fill remaining calories
- Rate of gain: slower for advanced lifters, faster for novices

## Benefits

- Supports maximal hypertrophy stimulus when training is progressive
- Improves recovery and training performance versus chronic deficits
- Allows clearer strength progression for many lifters

## Common Mistakes

- Massive surpluses that mostly increase fat mass
- Bulking without progressive training tracking
- Neglecting protein while “eating big”
- Never exiting a bulk to assess body composition

## Example Foods

- Higher-calorie mixed meals: rice, potatoes, pasta, meats, dairy, oils, dried fruit
- Protein staples kept consistent while carbs/fats rise
- Liquid calories if appetite is limiting

## Practical Tips

- Estimate maintenance, add a small surplus, then adjust every 1–2 weeks using weight and waist trends
- Favor mostly whole foods with flexible room for preferences
- Continue progressive overload; surplus without training stimulus wastes the phase
- Switch to maintenance or a cut when fat gain exceeds the plan

## Evidence Summary

Energy surplus supports hypertrophy when resistance training and protein intake are adequate, especially in novices. Larger surpluses increase fat gain disproportionately in trained lifters. Evidence supports controlled surpluses over extreme overfeeding for quality mass gain.
