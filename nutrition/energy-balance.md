# Energy Balance

## Overview

Energy balance is the relationship between calories consumed and calories expended. It is the primary determinant of long-term weight change and the framework behind bulking, cutting, and maintenance.

## Definition

Positive energy balance (surplus) tends to increase body mass; negative balance (deficit) tends to decrease body mass; equilibrium maintains mass when averaged over time. Body-composition change also depends on training and protein intake.

## Daily Recommendation

- Establish approximate maintenance using trend weight and logged intake
- Move to surplus for prioritized muscle gain or deficit for prioritized fat loss
- Adjust based on weekly averages, not daily noise
- Recalculate after body-mass or activity changes

## Benefits

- Provides a clear model for fat-loss and weight-gain planning
- Prevents magical thinking about “metabolism hacks” replacing intake control
- Helps separate water/glycogen shifts from true tissue change

## Common Mistakes

- Treating estimated TDEE calculators as perfect
- Ignoring NEAT reductions during dieting
- Expecting exact day-to-day scale precision
- Confusing diet composition effects on hunger with violations of energy balance

## Example Foods

- Any foods can fit mathematically; diet quality still affects health and adherence
- Protein-forward templates make deficits easier
- Higher carbohydrate availability supports hard training during either phase

## Practical Tips

- Track a 7-day average of weight and intake
- Change one major variable at a time (calories, steps, or training volume)
- Use waist measurements and photos alongside the scale
- Plan phase length before starting

## Evidence Summary

Human energy-balance research confirms that sustained mismatches between intake and expenditure change body energy stores. Macronutrients and food quality modulate hunger, performance, and lean-mass retention, but they operate within energy balance—not outside it.
