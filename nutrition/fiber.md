# Fiber

## Overview

Dietary fiber is the indigestible carbohydrate fraction of plant foods that supports digestive health, satiety, and cardiometabolic risk markers. It is a diet-quality target for lifters, not a direct muscle-building nutrient.

## Definition

Fiber includes soluble and insoluble types found in vegetables, fruits, legumes, and whole grains. Humans lack enzymes to fully digest these carbohydrates, so they affect gut function and fermentation.

## Daily Recommendation

- General adult targets commonly approximate ~14 g per 1,000 kcal or roughly 25–38 g/day depending on guidance and sex/energy needs
- Increase gradually with adequate fluids
- During hard cuts, fiber-rich foods improve fullness if GI tolerance allows

## Benefits

- Supports bowel regularity for many people
- Increases satiety during fat loss
- Associated with improved cardiometabolic markers in population and clinical research
- Improves overall diet quality when coming from diverse plants

## Common Mistakes

- Jumping intake dramatically and causing GI distress
- Relying only on powders while ignoring plant variety
- Cutting all high-fiber foods pre-contest without a plan, then rebounding poorly
- Assuming fiber cancels calories from overeating

## Example Foods

- Vegetables, berries, apples, pears
- Oats, barley, whole grains
- Beans, lentils, chickpeas
- Chia, flax, and other seeds

## Practical Tips

- Build each meal around a protein source plus produce
- Raise fiber by 5 g/day increments if bloating occurs
- Keep hydration up when increasing fiber
- Time lower-fiber meals around sensitive hard workouts if needed

## Evidence Summary

Public-health and clinical nutrition evidence strongly supports fiber for health and satiety. Sports performance effects are indirect via weight management, GI comfort, and diet quality rather than acute ergogenesis.
