# Intermittent Fasting

## Overview

Intermittent fasting (IF) restricts eating to certain hours or days. For body composition, IF mainly works by helping some people sustain a calorie deficit. It is not inherently superior to conventional meal patterns when calories and protein are equated.

## Definition

IF includes time-restricted eating (for example 16:8), alternate-day approaches, and related schedules that extend fasting intervals beyond a typical overnight fast.

## Daily Recommendation

- If using IF, still hit protein (~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day for many lifters) inside the eating window
- Match calories to fat-loss, maintenance, or surplus goals
- Place training where energy and protein availability remain adequate
- Stop protocols that impair training quality, sleep, or disordered eating risk

## Benefits

- May simplify calorie control for some adherents
- Can reduce evening snacking opportunities
- Produces fat loss comparable to continuous restriction when energy deficits match

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming IF burns fat independent of calorie balance
- Crushing protein intake into too few feedings and missing daily targets
- Training hard in a prolonged fasted state when performance suffers
- Using IF to justify nutrient-poor feasting windows

## Example Foods

- Same high-protein foods as other diet patterns, compressed into the feeding window
- Pre- and post-workout meals scheduled inside eating hours
- Fiber-rich plants for satiety during restricted schedules

## Practical Tips

- Choose a window compatible with training times
- Use 2–4 protein feedings inside the window when possible
- Compare adherence and strength to a regular meal pattern before committing long-term
- Prefer clinical guidance if there is a history of disordered eating

## Evidence Summary

RCTs and reviews generally show similar weight-loss outcomes for IF versus continuous energy restriction when calories are matched. Hypertrophy concerns center on practical protein distribution and training fuel, not mystical fasting pathways. Evidence is moderate for equivalence, not superiority.
