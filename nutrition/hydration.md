# Hydration

## Overview

Hydration status affects training performance, cognition, and heat tolerance. Most lifters need consistent daily fluid intake plus extra replacement around heavy sweating—not constant overdrinking.

## Definition

Hydration refers to body water balance. Dehydration is a body-water deficit that can impair performance; overhydration with inadequate sodium replacement can be risky in ultra-endurance settings.

## Daily Recommendation

- Begin with thirst, pale-yellow urine, and stable performance as practical checks
- ACSM-style guidance supports individualized fluid plans based on sweat rate for long/hot sessions
- Replace fluids across the day; add electrolytes when sweat losses are large

## Benefits

- Helps maintain stroke volume, thermoregulation, and high-intensity performance
- Reduces heat-related performance decline risk
- Supports training quality in hot or high-volume conditions

## Common Mistakes

- Waiting until severe thirst during long hot sessions
- Overdrinking plain water in ultra events without sodium planning
- Ignoring that coffee/tea still contribute to fluid intake for most habitual users
- Assuming everyone needs sports drinks for a 45-minute lift

## Example Foods

- Water, milk, and other fluids
- Water-rich fruits and vegetables
- Electrolyte beverages for prolonged sweaty training

## Practical Tips

- Drink regularly through the day rather than chugging only at the gym
- Weigh before/after long sessions to estimate sweat loss when precision matters
- Increase fluids with high-fiber diet changes
- Use electrolytes for long heat sessions, not every short workout by default

## Evidence Summary

ACSM hydration position stands show that dehydration can impair performance, especially in heat and prolonged exercise. Fluid needs are individual. Sports drinks are tools for long-duration fueling and electrolyte replacement, not mandatory for all resistance sessions.
