# Cutting

## Overview

Cutting is a planned fat-loss phase using a calorie deficit while preserving as much muscle and training performance as possible through high protein intake and continued resistance training.

## Definition

Cutting means sustained energy intake below maintenance with body-composition goals focused on reducing fat mass. It is the applied fat-loss phase counterpart to bulking.

## Daily Recommendation

- Use a moderate deficit, often ~300–750 kcal/day below maintenance depending on size and timeline
- Keep protein high (often ~1.6–2.4 g/kg/day in leaner or aggressive phases)
- Maintain hard resistance training; add cardio as needed for energy expenditure and preference
- Aim for gradual weekly fat loss rather than crash rates

## Benefits

- Reduces body fat while retaining more lean mass than diet-only approaches
- Improves visibility of muscle developed in prior growth phases
- Can improve metabolic health markers associated with excess adiposity when done sustainably

## Common Mistakes

- Deficits so large that strength, recovery, and adherence collapse
- Dropping protein and lifting volume
- Expecting linear scale weight daily without accounting for water/glycogen
- Endless cutting without maintenance phases

## Example Foods

- High-protein lean meats, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes
- High-volume vegetables and fruit for satiety
- Controlled portions of carbs around training
- Lower energy-dense swaps for oils, sugary drinks, and large snack calories

## Practical Tips

- Track average weekly weight and strength trends
- Keep steps and sleep consistent before adding extreme cardio
- Use diet breaks if fatigue and adherence worsen
- Stop cutting when performance or health markers deteriorate beyond the plan

## Evidence Summary

Fat loss requires a calorie deficit. Resistance training plus higher protein improves lean-mass retention during weight loss compared with dieting alone. Moderate deficits generally outperform crash diets for adherence and muscle retention.
