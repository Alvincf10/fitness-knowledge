# Body Recomposition

## Overview

Body recomposition means gaining muscle and losing fat at the same time. It is most realistic for beginners, people returning after layoffs, those with higher body fat, or trainees improving protein intake and training quality—less so for advanced lean lifters.

## Definition

Recomposition is a concurrent increase in fat-free mass and decrease in fat mass over a phase, often near energy maintenance or in a mild deficit with high protein and progressive resistance training.

## Daily Recommendation

- Set calories near maintenance or in a mild deficit
- Keep protein high (~1.6–2.2+ g/kg/day)
- Train progressively with sufficient weekly volume
- Use measurements beyond scale weight (strength, photos, circumference, optionally DEXA)

## Benefits

- Improves body composition without dedicated bulk/cut cycles for appropriate populations
- Reinforces habits: high protein, progressive lifting, step consistency
- Can be psychologically easier than aggressive cutting for some beginners

## Common Mistakes

- Expecting rapid dual progress when already advanced and lean
- Judging only by scale weight during water/glycogen shifts
- Under-eating protein while “maintaining”
- Changing programs too often to track progressive overload

## Example Foods

- High-protein maintenance-style meals
- Carb intake matched to training performance
- Fiber-rich plants for satiety without huge surpluses

## Practical Tips

- Track strength trends on key lifts
- Aim for stable weekly weight with improving measurements
- Give a recomposition block 8–12+ weeks before declaring failure
- Switch to surplus or deficit if progress stalls for your training age

## Evidence Summary

Research and applied reviews support concurrent fat loss and muscle gain under favorable conditions (novice status, overweight baseline, optimized protein, solid training). Advanced natural lifters usually need sequenced surplus/deficit phases for maximal rates of either outcome. Evidence is moderate and population-dependent.
