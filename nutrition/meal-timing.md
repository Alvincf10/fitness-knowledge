# Meal Timing

## Overview

Meal timing refers to when protein, carbohydrate, and calories are consumed relative to workouts and across the day. Timing can refine performance and recovery, but daily totals usually matter more for long-term body composition.

## Definition

Nutrient timing includes pre-, during-, and post-exercise intake plus the spacing of protein feedings across waking hours. It is a secondary optimization layer after energy balance and macronutrient targets.

## Daily Recommendation

- Meet daily protein and energy targets first
- For hypertrophy, distribute protein over 3–5 meals when practical
- Place carbohydrate around demanding sessions when training quality or glycogen matters
- Extreme anabolic-window urgency is unnecessary if daily intake is solid

## Benefits

- Supports workout performance with pre-session fuel
- Improves glycogen restoration when sessions are close together
- Helps adherence through scheduled meals
- Protein distribution can better support repeated MPS peaks

## Common Mistakes

- Obsessing over a 30-minute post-workout window while missing daily protein
- Training fasted and underfueled when performance is the priority
- Skipping meals then overeating chaotically at night without a plan
- Copying endurance fueling schedules for short lifting sessions unnecessarily

## Example Foods

- Pre-workout: yogurt and fruit, toast and eggs, rice and lean meat
- Post-workout: protein plus carbohydrate meal or shake plus food
- Evenly spaced protein meals across the day

## Practical Tips

- Pick a meal pattern you can repeat most days
- If training early, use a small digestible carb/protein snack if a full meal is unrealistic
- For late sessions, keep pre-workout meals moderate to limit GI issues
- Use timing as fine-tuning after adherence to totals is consistent

## Evidence Summary

Reviews indicate that while peri-workout nutrition can matter acutely, hypertrophy is driven mainly by total protein and progressive training. ISSN nutrient-timing literature supports practical peri-exercise feeding without requiring rigid minute-by-minute rules for most recreational lifters.
