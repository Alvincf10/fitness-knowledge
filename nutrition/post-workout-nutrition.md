# Post-Workout Nutrition

## Overview

Post-workout nutrition helps meet daily protein needs and restores carbohydrate when sessions are demanding or closely spaced. The old “anabolic window closes in 30 minutes” claim overstates urgency for most fed trainees.

## Definition

Post-workout nutrition is the meal or snack after training that contributes to recovery, muscle protein synthesis, and glycogen restoration as part of total daily intake.

## Daily Recommendation

- Consume a protein-rich feeding within a few hours after training as part of daily distribution
- Add carbohydrate when glycogen restoration or another session comes soon
- Daily protein and energy targets still dominate hypertrophy outcomes
- Rapid feeding matters more if training fasted or if the next session is within hours

## Benefits

- Supports MPS when contributing to daily protein total
- Speeds glycogen recovery for multi-session days
- Improves adherence by anchoring a consistent recovery meal habit

## Common Mistakes

- Panic-shaking protein within minutes while skipping later meals
- Assuming a shake replaces total daily protein needs
- Overeating “reward” calories that erase a cut
- Ignoring fluids after heavy sweat losses

## Example Foods

- Whey shake plus fruit
- Chicken and rice
- Greek yogurt and granola
- Eggs and potatoes

## Practical Tips

- Plan the post-workout meal before training when schedule is tight
- Use shakes for convenience, food for satiety and micronutrients
- Scale carbs to session difficulty and next-day training
- Rehydrate based on thirst and sweat loss

## Evidence Summary

Reviews show that while peri-workout protein can be useful, total daily protein and training stimulus explain most hypertrophy variance. Carbohydrate after exercise is most important when rapid glycogen restoration is needed. ISSN timing literature supports practical, not obsessive, post-workout feeding.
