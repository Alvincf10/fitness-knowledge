# Calorie Surplus

## Overview

A calorie surplus occurs when average intake exceeds expenditure. In resistance trainees, a controlled surplus can support faster muscle gain, especially in beginners, at the cost of some fat gain.

## Definition

Calorie surplus = energy intake − energy expenditure over time. Surpluses can be modest or large; size influences the ratio of lean to fat gain.

## Daily Recommendation

- Use the smallest surplus that still supports measurable progress (often ~250–500 kcal/day for many intermediates)
- Keep protein high and training progressive
- Smaller surpluses for advanced lifters with slower growth potential
- Monitor waist and strength to guide adjustments

## Benefits

- Improves recovery and training capacity versus chronic deficits
- Supports hypertrophy when stimulus and protein are present
- Helps high-energy athletes meet fuel demands

## Common Mistakes

- Equating surplus size with muscle-gain speed linearly at advanced levels
- Dirty bulking without tracking composition
- Surplus without progressive overload
- Ignoring that novices can gain muscle near maintenance more easily than advanced lifters

## Example Foods

- Calorie-dense additions: rice, oats, oils, nut butters, dried fruit, dairy
- Protein kept stable while carbs/fats increase
- Liquid calories if chewing appetite is limiting

## Practical Tips

- Confirm true maintenance before adding surplus calories
- Increase gradually and review biweekly
- Keep most foods nutrient-dense
- Exit to maintenance if fat gain outpaces the plan

## Evidence Summary

Energy availability influences hypertrophy rate. Research and applied reviews support modest surpluses for leaner gains in trained individuals, while larger surpluses mainly increase fat deposition once growth capacity is constrained.
