# Calorie Deficit

## Overview

A calorie deficit occurs when average energy intake is below average energy expenditure. It is the required condition for net fat loss, regardless of diet style.

## Definition

Calorie deficit = energy expenditure − energy intake over time. Expenditure includes basal metabolism, thermic effect of food, exercise, and non-exercise activity.

## Daily Recommendation

- Create a moderate deficit appropriate to body size and timeline (often a few hundred kcal/day)
- Prioritize protein and resistance training to protect lean mass
- Recalculate as body mass and activity change
- Avoid chronic extreme deficits that destroy training quality

## Benefits

- Produces fat loss when sustained
- Can improve health markers linked to excess adiposity
- Works with many dietary patterns if adherence and protein are managed

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming a special food combination beats energy balance
- Underestimating intake and overestimating expenditure
- Pairing huge deficits with excessive cardio and poor recovery
- Confusing short-term water/glycogen loss with fat loss

## Example Foods

- Any pattern that hits protein and micronutrient needs at a lower average calorie level
- High-volume vegetables, lean proteins, measured fats
- Reduced liquid calories and low-satiety snacks

## Practical Tips

- Track trends weekly, not single weigh-ins
- Use steps, sleep, and strength as recovery guardrails
- Tighten logging accuracy before cutting calories further
- Consider diet breaks when adherence falls

## Evidence Summary

Obesity and sports-nutrition literature agree that fat loss requires negative energy balance. Macronutrient distribution and food quality affect hunger, lean-mass retention, and health, but they do not override thermodynamics.
